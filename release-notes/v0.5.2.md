Every other flag and victory value is the same as in `v0.5.1`. `aiEconomy` means: under `classical-faithful` the AI recruits, taxes and fortifies as the original's decompiled turn does; under `improved` it keeps the clone's own affordability-scored economy. T156 (the army target tree) applies to both presets; T158 and T159 (fleet order, consolidation, merges) apply under `classical-faithful` only.

The `ai` block of all three rulesets (`classical-faithful`, `improved`, `toy-ruleset`) gains **75 new values** (T156–T159), identical in the three files: the target tree's thresholds and scores, the economy's threat budget, order limits, tax rules and recruit-type draw, and the fleet order and merge gates. A ruleset file that gives such a value a bound the game would crash on is now refused at load (§3, #943 and #973).

**Known caveat, unchanged:** `flags.diplomacyModel` is read by no code (#472, open). The two presets play diplomacy the same way, whatever this row says.

## 3. What changed for a player

### The AI now plays to conquer

- **AI armies follow the original's target tree** (T156, #925; both presets). At war, an army chooses between attacking an enemy city, attacking an enemy army, going to resupply, or hiring mercenaries, by the scores the original uses `[confirmed: decompile]`; an army whose chosen action fails falls back to guarding a city, so none stands idle with moves left. This replaces the clone's own `[designed]` siege weights, which `v0.5.1` called "too timid". Expect AI nations to take cities now, and to come for yours.
- **Under `classical-faithful`, the AI's economy is the original's** (T157, #926). It recruits for a "threat budget" (more recruits when it is at war with many nations or enemy armies are near its capital), places at most 8 orders a turn, changes its tax rate only in week 11 by three rules, and **never orders a fortification**. The recruit types and amounts follow the original's draw. Under `improved` the AI keeps the clone's own economy.
- **Under `classical-faithful`, the AI orders fleets and merges its forces** (T158, #958; T159, #959). It orders at most one new fleet a turn by the original's gates (fewer than 99 fleets in the game, treasury above 3000, a fleet score of 0, or 1 or 2 with more than 50 or 100 cities), consolidates its army units, and merges a small army or fleet into a nearby one of its own, at most one army merge and one fleet merge a turn per nation. A merged unit keeps everything (troops, supplies, money by the purse rules); a unit record holding no troops is dropped, as the original does (the user's decision 6 on T159). A merge writes no news line, as in the original.
- **An AI fleet no longer plans a sail into an occupied tile** (#929, the fleet side of `v0.5.1`'s #907 fix).
- **Still `[designed]` or unchecked:** the AI under `improved`; T159's "once per round" is `[derived]` from the seat loop; the original's in-play corrections to the intercept and fleet-hunt rules are a Wine candidate, not yet checked.

### Rules, windows and wording

- **Transfer ships that empties a fleet adds its money to the other fleet whole** (T160, #966), as the original does (research Td0–Td2). It is no longer capped at 1,000 with the excess paid into the treasury. The money field's own limit of 32,767 still applies and is `[designed]`: a larger sum saturates and the excess is discarded.
- **A ruleset with a bad AI divisor or random-draw bound is refused at load** (#943, #973), with a message naming the key, instead of crashing later in the AI's turn. Fourteen `ai.*` divisor keys and the random-draw bounds (including `ai.faithfulTypeDrawBound`) must be greater than 0.
- **International Relations can be closed** (#968). Esc closes it, and the Close button stays on screen with sixteen nations and a pending offer.
- **Make Peace is enabled for trade, alliance and war** (#915), as the engine accepts it. It was enabled only at war.
- **The AI's trade or alliance offer shows the original's OK box** at the start of your turn (#917), for example "Seleucid wants to trade with Rome." The user decided that the box is a notice only: **Accept Offer in International Relations stays the way to accept**, and the box writes no news line and changes no relation.
- **Dialog wordings** (#914). Taxation, Build fleet and the fleet dialogs no longer show the engine's raw "accepted" text. Build fleet says "The fleet will be built at <city>." (the original's wording). The original closes the other dialogs silently; the short sentences "Tax set.", "Fleets split.", "Fleets joined.", "Fleet supplied.", "Ships transferred." and "Mercenaries hired." are the user's choice of 2026-10-11.
- **Fleet news lines name the nation** (#916): "A fleet belonging to Carthage is lost at sea", not `carthage`; likewise for a fleet finished or damaged in a storm.
- **The window's close button asks first, and Save writes back** (#919). With a game open, closing the window shows the same prompt as File → Close and quits only on Yes; on the title screen it quits at once. After File → Open, Save rewrites the file you opened; a new game never saved keeps its `<scenario>-turn-<N>.json` name.
- **The fall texts** (#921). The conquered text names the conqueror by its name, not its id ("Your nation has been conquerred by Northern League."; "conquerred" is the original's spelling, kept). The watch-mode notice now says why you were handed to the AI: all cities held, the 20-year limit, overthrown by the army, deposed by an unpaid army, or conquered. That wording is the clone's own `[designed]`; the original's text for it is not known. **Loading a save now runs the fall check at once**, so a save whose human seat already meets a fall condition falls on load, not a turn later.

Carried over from `v0.5.1`, still `[designed]`: `improved`'s three rows and its `combat.onDefeat` scatter, the legality-first attack, the delayed battle report, the purse caps, the mercenary fill's own random stream, the leader draw, the painted map surface, the generated sounds, the new dialogs' unread wording, and the recruitment gate's refusal message.

## 7. Artifacts and how to run them

- **Archive:** `ImperialConquest2-v0.5.2-windows-x64.zip` (SIZE; SHA-256 HASH). Extract it anywhere and run `ic2\game\IC2.MapViewer.exe`; no Godot or .NET SDK is needed.

From source you need the **.NET 10 SDK** and **Godot 4.7.2 (.NET build)**: build `godot/IC2.MapViewer.csproj`, then run the Godot project (main scene `res://UI/AppRoot.tscn`); or run the CLI with `dotnet run --project src/IC2.Cli`.

**Local-only:** only original-`.sav` import and the data tests that read real saves need your original installation (`assets.local.ini`). No original game file is part of this repository or this release.
