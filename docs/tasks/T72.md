- **Scope**: `PurseAccounting.Credit` returns `min(PurseCapPerUnit, current + amount)` for **every** call, debits included. It is called by:
  - the join-armies command, which caps the joined purse at 1,000 (`JoinArmiesCommandHandler.cs:78`), and its naval twin, the join-fleets command (`JoinFleetsCommandHandler.cs:91`);
  - the fleet-to-fleet transfer, both directions (`FleetToFleetTransferCommandHandler.cs:134`, `:172`);
  - supply purchase, as a debit (`SupplyPurchase.cs:183`, `:272`);
  - automatic resupply, as a debit (`AutomaticResupply.cs:144`, `:229`), which also sweeps any excess over the cap to the treasury (`:251–254`), and as a **positive** credit, the own-city top-up grant (`:265`), which only a purse under `autoResupplyPurseTopUpThreshold` (500) receives, so `Credit`'s cap never binds there;
  - the treasury-to-purse transfer (`TreasuryPurseTransfer.cs:89`, `:109`).

  One writer adds money **without** `Credit`, despite `PurseAccounting.cs`'s remark that it is "the one place" a purse is credited: battle absorption, `InstantBattleResolver.cs:183` (`Money = winner.Money + absorbedMoney`), which is already uncapped. Done-when 1 cites it and changes nothing, and `PurseAccounting.cs`'s remark is corrected to name it.

  The evidence says the cap is **one write path's clamp**, not a property of the record:
  - `purseCapPerUnit` (1,000) traces to `TAFSupply_ChangeMoney`, the supply dialog's input clamp ([`decompiled-unit-map-orders-and-record-fields.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/main/docs/reports/decompiled-unit-map-orders-and-record-fields.md));
  - the same report's Join-armies row says "supplies and money add", and its Join-fleets row (~:79) "ships, supplies and money add", both with no cap;
  - a real save holds an army purse of **1,066** (`IP016.sav`, army 1; bug #315, and the range sweep `tests/IC2.Data.Tests/CorpusFixtures/ArmyFleetFieldRangeSweepTests.cs` ~:44–56 — no research report records it);
  - the field is floored at 0 ([`upkeep-payment-and-desertion.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/main/docs/reports/upkeep-payment-and-desertion.md), `00451c1c`, a signed compare); "signed 16-bit, `0 … 32,767`" is inferred from that compare and the range sweep (~:40–58), and Done-when 4 treats it so.

  So a purse above 1,000 is legitimate. After T21 it is routine, through imports. Today, any such purse is silently cut to 1,000 on its next purchase or resupply, on top of the cost.

  **Every purse write of the original, from [`2026-10-05-army-purse-writes-and-the-1000-cap.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/9ae8924/docs/reports/2026-10-05-army-purse-writes-and-the-1000-cap.md)** (research `9ae8924`, provisional; `[derived]` = its decompile reading, the play results Wine candidates). It settles Done-when 1 for the army writers; cite its row for each:
  - **Only three paths cap at 1,000**: the Supply army money arrows and the Army to army money arrows, both `min(step, 1000 − receiver)` with **no floor at 0**, and the AI's own-city refill (rows 1, 4, 10) **[derived]**. Every other add is uncapped (row 3, 5b, 7, 12, 13).
  - **Join armies** adds the purses with no cap, as a 16-bit add (`TUnitMap_JoinArmies` :46992-46993, row 7) **[derived]**; 1,000 + 1,000 gave **2,000** **[Wine candidate: `Q1_05_before_join.SAV` → `Q1_06_after_join.SAV`]**. This is the path that yields a purse such as IP016's 1,066.
  - **The dialog's money arrow pulls an over-cap purse back**: from 2,000, one "+100" click moved −1,000, so the purse fell to 1,000 and the treasury rose by 1,000 **[Wine candidate: `Q1b_01_after_one_up_click.SAV`]**. The clone's `TreasuryPurseTransfer` applies nothing in that case: bug [#757](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial_conquest_2/issues/757), whose file is in this task's Owns; triage decides whether it folds in here.
  - **Buy supplies** (row 3) only subtracts on a positive amount, never below 0, but an over-full army's room is negative, so the amount is negative and the purse **rises** by `−amount div 5` with no cap: 10,000 troops, 206 supplies and purse 1,000 give room `100 − 206 + 1 = −105`, and the purse becomes **1,021** **[derived]**.
  - **Automatic resupply** (row 10): at an own city, a purse above 1,000 sends the excess to the treasury and becomes 1,000; then a purse under 500 with a treasury above 0 gains 500 from it. At a foreign city the purse pays `tons div 5`, and a negative `tons` (an over-full army) raises it with no cap **[derived]**. The sweep is the original's, so it stays. Only the AI reaches this path in the original; the clone's human trigger is bug [#756](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial_conquest_2/issues/756), outside this task.
  - **A battle's captured purse** adds uncapped (row 12, 12b) **[derived]**, as `InstantBattleResolver.cs:183` already does.
  - **Not settled by it**: Join fleets and the fleet-to-fleet transfer (`TFleetToFleet`). The report's row 2 caps a **fleet** purse at 1,000 only on the Supply army dialog's down arrow (`min(step, 1000 − fleet's purse)`) **[derived]**. Done-when 1's research check still applies to these two.
- **Done when**:
  1. **Research first.** For each writer above, the task states, with a citation to a report or a decompiled address, whether the original caps that write at `PurseCapPerUnit`. For any writer the evidence does not settle, a targeted research check (`all_app_functions.txt`, then Ghidra) is run and its result recorded in the PR. A writer still unsettled after that keeps today's behaviour, marked `[open]` in its remark with what was searched. It is never changed on a guess.
  2. **The cap applies exactly where the original applies it.** At minimum, the supply dialog's treasury-to-purse transfer keeps its clamp. A debit never raises or cuts a purse beyond the amount debited, and a negative debit (the giveback of an over-full army) raises it uncapped. An army join adds without a cap (the report above); a fleet join does unless step 1 finds a cap. The own-city resupply sweep of excess to the treasury is kept (row 10).
  3. **Tests, one per writer**, each with a purse above 1,000 going in:
     - a purchase from a 1,066 purse leaves exactly `1,066 − cost`;
     - a paid purchase by an over-full army (10,000 troops, 206 supplies, purse 1,000) leaves the purse at 1,021 (the report's row 3);
     - a join of 800 + 600 gives 1,400 for armies, and for fleets unless step 1 finds a cap;
     - a fleet-to-fleet transfer in each direction into a purse near or over 1,000;
     - an own-city resupply of a 1,066 purse leaves 1,000 and moves 66 to the treasury, and one of a 499 purse with a positive treasury leaves 999;
     - the dialog transfer still stops at 1,000.

     Each test fails if its path is reverted to today's behaviour (prove by mutation).
  4. **No purse leaves the field's range**: `0 … 32,767`, the signed 16-bit read. Any add that could exceed it states what the original does (a wrap or a clamp) with evidence, or is marked `[open]`. The army join and the captured purse are 16-bit adds, so the original wraps above 32,767 (the report's rows 7 and 12) **[derived: code, not played]**.
  5. **`economy.purseCapPerUnit`'s `_provenance`** says it is the supply dialog's clamp, not a record cap, in all three rulesets, through the exporter.
  6. `dotnet build IC2.sln` and `dotnet test IC2.sln` green, and the diff lists only Owns paths.
- **Hazards**:
  - **Don't "fix" a cap by moving it.** A clamp relocated to a different writer is the same defect in a new place. Step 1 decides where each cap belongs.
  - **Existing tests pinned to a capped result** are evidence of the bug, not of correct behaviour. Recompute them from the path's real rule; never widen them into a range.
  - **Money is conserved.** Wherever a cap stays, say where the excess goes (the treasury, or nowhere), and test it.
  - Bug #315 was filed from the T64 review; T21's import preserves saved purses as they are, so this task makes their later spending correct.
