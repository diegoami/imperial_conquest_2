#### T72 Cap a purse only where the original does

- **Design milestone**: M3 (economy), a correction — bug [#315](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial_conquest_2/issues/315). **Labels**: `phase:2 lane:engine`
- **Branch**: `task/T72-purse-cap` · **Model/effort**: Sonnet / Medium · **Reviewer**: **Opus / Medium**

  - its callers, **only their purse-write lines and remarks**: `src/IC2.Engine/Economy/SupplyPurchase.cs`, `src/IC2.Engine/Economy/AutomaticResupply.cs`, `src/IC2.Engine/Economy/TreasuryPurseTransfer.cs`, `src/IC2.Engine/Armies/Commands/JoinArmiesCommandHandler.cs`, `src/IC2.Engine/Armies/Commands/JoinArmiesCommand.cs` (remarks only), `src/IC2.Engine/Naval/Commands/JoinFleetsCommandHandler.cs`, `src/IC2.Engine/Naval/Commands/FleetToFleetTransferCommandHandler.cs`

  - tests: `tests/IC2.Engine.Tests/Economy/PurseAccountingTests.cs`, `AutomaticResupplyTests.cs`, `tests/IC2.Engine.Tests/Economy/Commands/**` (supply and treasury-transfer tests), the join-armies tests under `tests/IC2.Engine.Tests/Armies/**`, and `tests/IC2.Engine.Tests/Naval/JoinAndSplitFleetsCommandHandlerTests.cs` and `FleetToFleetTransferCommandHandlerTests.cs`
- **Scope**: `PurseAccounting.Credit` returns `min(PurseCapPerUnit, current + amount)` for **every** call, debits included. It is called by:
  - the join-armies command, which caps the joined purse at 1,000 (`JoinArmiesCommandHandler.cs:78`), and its naval twin, the join-fleets command (`JoinFleetsCommandHandler.cs:91`);
  - the fleet-to-fleet transfer, both directions (`FleetToFleetTransferCommandHandler.cs:134`, `:172`);
  - supply purchase, as a debit (`SupplyPurchase.cs:183`, `:272`);
  - automatic resupply, as a debit (`AutomaticResupply.cs:144`, `:229`), which also sweeps any excess over the cap to the treasury (`:251–254`), and as a **positive** credit, the own-city top-up grant (`:265`: a purse at 900 given 500 is cut to 1,000);
  - the treasury-to-purse transfer (`TreasuryPurseTransfer.cs:89`, `:109`).

  One writer adds money **without** `Credit`, despite `PurseAccounting.cs`'s remark that it is "the one place" a purse is credited: battle absorption, `InstantBattleResolver.cs:183` (`Money = winner.Money + absorbedMoney`), which is already uncapped. Done-when 1 cites it and changes nothing, and `PurseAccounting.cs`'s remark is corrected to name it.

  The evidence says the cap is **one write path's clamp**, not a property of the record:
  - `purseCapPerUnit` (1,000) traces to `TAFSupply_ChangeMoney`, the supply dialog's input clamp ([`decompiled-unit-map-orders-and-record-fields.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/main/docs/reports/decompiled-unit-map-orders-and-record-fields.md));
  - the same report's Join-armies row says "supplies and money add", and its Join-fleets row (~:79) "ships, supplies and money add", both with no cap;
  - a real save holds an army purse of **1,066** (`IP016.sav`, army 1; bug #315, and the range sweep `tests/IC2.Data.Tests/CorpusFixtures/ArmyFleetFieldRangeSweepTests.cs` ~:44–56 — no research report records it);
  - the field is floored at 0 ([`upkeep-payment-and-desertion.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/main/docs/reports/upkeep-payment-and-desertion.md), `00451c1c`, a signed compare); "signed 16-bit, `0 … 32,767`" is inferred from that compare and the range sweep (~:40–58), and Done-when 4 treats it so.

  So a purse above 1,000 is legitimate. After T21 it is routine, through imports. Today, any such purse is silently cut to 1,000 on its next purchase or resupply, on top of the cost.
- **Done when**:
  1. **Research first.** For each writer above, the task states, with a citation to a report or a decompiled address, whether the original caps that write at `PurseCapPerUnit`. For any writer the evidence does not settle, a targeted research check (`all_app_functions.txt`, then Ghidra) is run and its result recorded in the PR. A writer still unsettled after that keeps today's behaviour, marked `[open]` in its remark with what was searched. It is never changed on a guess.
  2. **The cap applies exactly where the original applies it.** At minimum, the supply dialog's treasury-to-purse transfer keeps its clamp. A debit never raises or cuts a purse beyond the amount debited. A join adds without a cap unless step 1 finds one. The resupply sweep of excess to the treasury is kept or removed per step 1.
  3. **Tests, one per writer**, each with a purse above 1,000 going in:
     - a purchase from a 1,066 purse leaves exactly `1,066 − cost`;
     - a join of 800 + 600 gives 1,400, unless step 1 finds a cap, for armies **and** for fleets;
     - a fleet-to-fleet transfer in each direction into a purse near or over 1,000;
     - an own-city top-up grant into a 900 purse;
     - the dialog transfer still stops at 1,000.

     Each test fails if its path is reverted to today's behaviour (prove by mutation).
  4. **No purse leaves the field's range**: `0 … 32,767`, the signed 16-bit read. Any add that could exceed it states what the original does (a wrap or a clamp) with evidence, or is marked `[open]`.
  5. **`economy.purseCapPerUnit`'s `_provenance`** says it is the supply dialog's clamp, not a record cap, in all three rulesets, through the exporter.
  6. `dotnet build IC2.sln` and `dotnet test IC2.sln` green, and the diff lists only Owns paths.
- **Hazards**:
  - **Don't "fix" a cap by moving it.** A clamp relocated to a different writer is the same defect in a new place. Step 1 decides where each cap belongs.
  - **Existing tests pinned to a capped result** are evidence of the bug, not of correct behaviour. Recompute them from the path's real rule; never widen them into a range.
  - **Money is conserved.** Wherever a cap stays, say where the excess goes (the treasury, or nowhere), and test it.
  - Bug #315 was filed from the T64 review; T21's import preserves saved purses as they are, so this task makes their later spending correct.
