- **Scope**: Research [`decompiled-war-cascade-and-peace-paths.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/main/docs/reports/decompiled-war-cascade-and-peace-paths.md) (research `24d00b6`, **[confirmed: decompile]** unless tagged otherwise) is the source for every rule below.
  - **The cascade is one step (#383).** The setter `FUN_00449B40` writes each dragged-in war directly, and never calls itself.
    - Declaring war on `b` puts each ally of `b` at war with you.
    - Allying with `b` puts you at war with each enemy of `b`.
    - The cascade goes no further.

    The engine's `RelationTransitions.DeclareWar` recurses, and `FormAlliance` goes through it. With A–B, B–C and C–D allied, when X declares war on A, the original puts X at war with A and B, with two news lines. The engine puts X at war with A, B, C and D.
  - **An AI never ends a war with a human without the human's consent (#384).**
    - The AI's own diplomacy never writes peace over a war.
    - The human's Politics screen refuses to end a war with an AI. Ending a trade (−8) or an alliance (−24) is allowed.
    - The only path is the **post-battle treaty** (`TBattleOver_OK` → the `TBattlePols` dialog → `FUN_00450C68`). A battle involving a human offers it when all of these hold:
      - the winner's total army strength is below the loser's;
      - the loser's unity is above 500;
      - the loser holds more than 7 cities;
      - then `Random(5) < 2`, drawn only after the other tests pass.

      The human answers Yes or No. On Yes, the treaty always takes the honourable branch and never reparations. That part is **[derived]**: it rests on the dialog being modal.
    - The engine's AI proposes peace to a human through `ProposePeace`, which is always accepted. The original applies "always accepted" only between two humans.
  - **The peace cascade (`FUN_00450C68`, report §3)** handles each ally of the two sides. It:
    - resets that side's alliance with the ally to **−8**, unconditionally;
    - makes the ally join the peace only if it doesn't border the enemy and isn't human;
    - names the enemy first in the news line;
    - handles an ally's two halves together.

    `PeaceTreatySystem`'s loop differs on each point.
- **Done when**:
  1. **One-step cascade.** Using the chain of four allies above, one test shows X ends at war with A and B only, and one test shows the alliance cascade is one step as well. Each news line matches the setter's own line. The `DeclareWar` remark no longer says the setter calls itself.
  2. **No AI peace towards a human.**
     - `ProposePeace` never targets a human seat.
     - A test gives an AI a losing war with a human, and shows that no peace candidate is proposed.
     - Hotseat peace between two humans is still always accepted, and a test shows it.
  3. **The treaty with a human.**
     - A battle involving a human raises the treaty only through the four gates. There is a test per gate at its boundary: strength equal and one less, unity 500 and 501, 7 cities and 8. The `Random(5)` draw is not taken when an earlier gate fails.
     - The CLI shows the offer and accepts a yes or no answer.
     - Yes writes the honourable peace and its news, with no reparations; No writes nothing.
     - The AI-vs-AI treaty is unchanged, apart from the cascade fixes.
  4. **The peace cascade.** A test for each point of report §3, including the −8 reset and the border and human checks. The border check uses T85's neighbour query.
  5. **A human can end a trade or an alliance.** If `MakePeaceCommand` refuses anything but a war, extend the human's Politics path so a human can end a trade (−8) or an alliance (−24), as the report says. If some other command already does this, show it with a test instead.
  6. **Seeded outcomes.** Every seeded test, measurement or golden that changes is listed with its reason. `seat-rome.golden.txt` changes: its round-1 Gaul–Rome peace came through the removed path, as T82's review noted on #384. The 50-seed soak keeps 0 rejected commands.
  7. `dotnet build IC2.sln` and `dotnet test IC2.sln` green, and the diff lists only Owns paths.
- **Hazards**:
  - **The treaty dialog makes the battle wait.** The original is modal. In the engine, a battle that raises the treaty for a human must leave a pending decision the CLI can show, without blocking the AI's turn loop. Pick the smallest design that does this, say which one, and test that a No, or ignoring the offer, leaves the war in place.
  - **Random streams.** The original seeds the treaty's RNG with `winner + loser` and always draws `Random(W/4)`. Reproducing that exactly is **optional** here. If you don't, say so in the remark.
  - **Many outcomes move.** A one-step cascade means fewer wars, so AI outcomes change widely. List them rather than tuning anything.
