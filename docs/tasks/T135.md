- **Scope**:
  - **Protects**: an **accepted** attack, siege or fleet attack on a nation at peace still declares war first, exactly as today: the same `diplomacy.declare-war accepted (composed ahead of the attack).` line, the same relation writes, the same one-step cascade to allies (`RelationTransitions.DeclareWar`), the same news in the same order, and no random draw. `success.golden.txt` and `success-fleet.golden.txt`, and every other CLI golden, stay byte-identical. The standalone `declare-war` verb, the AI's attacks (`AiMilitaryPhase`, which composes no declaration), `AttackLegality` and every command handler are unchanged. Forbidden: an edit under `src/IC2.Engine/Battle/**`, `src/IC2.Engine/Diplomacy/**` or `src/IC2.Engine/Ai/**`; a new verb; a war left in place by a refused attack.
  - **What is wrong** **[confirmed: code and a run, #579]**. `ComposeDeclareWarIfNeeded` dispatches `DeclareWarCommand` and commits its state and news before the attack command runs its own legality check. When the attack is then refused, the war stays: T99's review probe (a peace-time attack on a foreign fleet carrying an army) printed `diplomacy.declare-war accepted`, then `battle.attack-army rejected (battle.target-embarked)`, and Rome–Carthage went from 0 to 3. The cascade carries it to the target's allies too ([`2026-10-03-fleet-peace-prompt.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/main/docs/reports/2026-10-03-fleet-peace-prompt.md)). Today's CLI shows it without any embarked target: `besiege-city army-0 misurata` from Rome's start (not adjacent) declares war on Carthage and is then refused with `battle.siege-not-adjacent` (`CommandOutcomeTextTests`, ~:70).
  - **The rule** **[designed: legality first, then war]**: the declaration and the attack commit **together or not at all**. When the two nations are not at war, the session declares the war on a candidate state and runs the attack against that candidate (so the attack's own war gate sees the war it needs); if the attack is accepted, both are committed, with today's lines, state and news; if it is refused, nothing of the declaration is committed: the state (relations, news log, every other field) is the state before the order, and the output is the attack's own rejection line alone, with no declaration line. The original's order is now read **[derived: code, `TUnitMap_SelectUnit` @ 004466CC; [`2026-10-05-refused-attack-declares-nothing.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/9ae8924/docs/reports/2026-10-05-refused-attack-declares-nothing.md), research `9ae8924`, provisional]**: **legality, then the prompt, then the declaration, then the attack**, and nothing refuses after the declaration.
    - For a city or army target, the click goes on only if an army is selected, has moves ≥ 1, stands at distance exactly 1, and the target belongs to another nation; otherwise there is no box and no war. At any relation but war (3) the box *"Are you sure you want to attack this city ?"* / *"…this army ?"* appears; on Yes, `FUN_00449B40` declares (both directions, then the target's allies, one step), then the siege or the field battle runs. Neither `FUN_0044B27C`, `FUN_0044AEE4` nor `FUN_0044B5D0` has a refusal branch.
    - For a fleet target the legality is the **selected fleet's** (moves ≥ 1, distance 1), and the target is refused with *"You cannot attack a fleet docked at its own city !"* **before any prompt** when its owner has a city anywhere in the 3 × 3 around it (`FUN_004494E4`, :46619-46639), which is what `AttackLegality.DockedAtOwnCity` already tests.
    - Played **[Wine candidate, the same report]**: Gaul's army clicking Greece's Genua 4 tiles away, and adjacent with 0 moves, showed no box and left the relation at 0 / 0 with no news; adjacent with moves, No changed nothing; Yes gave 3 / 3, *"GAUL DECLARES WAR ON GREECE."* and then the siege.

    So the original never declares a war that its attack then refuses; this rule gives the clone the same outcome for the refusals the original makes before its prompt. The clone's handlers also refuse cases the original has no branch for (an embarked target, say), and for those the rule is **[designed]**. The remarks say so, and cite #579.
  - **Unchanged around it**: the watch-mode and seat-lost gate still runs before anything is dispatched (review round 1, B2, in the method's remarks); no declaration is composed when the nations are already at war, when the target cannot be resolved, or when the target is the issuing nation; a `declare-war` that is itself refused still leaves the attack to report through its own gates.
  - **The Godot app needs no change of its own.** The attack prompt (`MapClickRules.ConfirmedAttack`) submits one order and lets the session compose the war; after this task a refused order's label shows the refusal alone. The two tests and the one check that assert a declaration ahead of a **refused** attack change with the rule: each keeps its point (the label shows the command's whole own outcome) by asserting the refusal alone for the refused case, and `An_attack_that_composes_a_declaration_of_war_shows_both_its_lines` moves to an **accepted** attack at peace, whose two lines are the declaration and the attack's acceptance.

- **Hazards**:
  - **Restore, don't recompute.** The simplest correct shape keeps the state from before the order and puts it back when the attack is refused. Anything `IssueCommand` sets on a refusal (none today: a refused command appends no news and captures no battle) must not leak; if the implementer finds a side effect that the restore misses, stop and report.
  - **The original's order is read, under Wine** (the 2026-10-05 report above): its refusals all come before the prompt, and the docked-fleet refusal is one of them. A difference the desktop original shows later is a follow-up, not a reason to hold this task.
  - **Don't touch `godot/UI/**`.** `CommandOutcomeText.cs`'s remarks describe why the label shows the whole outcome, citing the declaration ahead of a rejected attack as the round-1 case; that history stays true and is not this task's.
  - **Never two Godot processes at once**: run the headless checks one at a time.
