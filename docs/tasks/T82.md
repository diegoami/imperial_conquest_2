- **Scope**: [`decompiled-ai-offers-to-human-seats.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/main/docs/reports/decompiled-ai-offers-to-human-seats.md) (research `0d2674c` and `35ace81`) establishes the original's flow, and #357 lists where the engine differs. In short: The widened Done-when 3 also brings the AI's **own** per-turn diplomacy (`FUN_0044FB7C`) to the original's rule. Today the engine's AI allies with any nation at peace, which produced a cascade of about 38 alliances in the first round of the classical scenario (#357's comment).
  - **AI to AI:** set directly.
  - **AI to human:** an offer rolled at the human's turn start (`FUN_00452034`, under its conditions) is shown as an OK-only notice. The relation is never written, and the offer clears at the next human turn start.
  - **Accepting:** the human accepts through the ordinary proposal rules, and for an alliance with an AI only the human's side is checked.
  - **War:** the AI never declares war on an ally that holds a city, and its units attack only nations already at war.
- **Done when**:
  1. **An AI offer to a human is a notice.** An AI never writes a relation with a human seat directly. At the human's turn start, the original's roll (`FUN_00452034`: the proposer, the type, and the trade and alliance conditions, including the neighbour relation, from the report) may store a pending offer. The CLI shows it, and nothing else changes. It clears at the next human turn start. Tests: an offer rolled; the relation unchanged; the offer cleared a turn later.
  2. **Accepting follows the original.** A human's trade or alliance with an AI goes through the report's human-to-AI rules: the trade's three-partner exception for a pending offer, and **only the human's side** checked for an alliance. That changes `AcceptPendingOfferCommand`'s two-sided check. Tests pin each condition, and each fails if the condition is deleted.
  3. **The AI's own diplomacy is `FUN_0044FB7C` (widened 2026-09-25 by the user's decision; the user found the gap playing the classical scenario, #357's comment).** On its turn, an AI writes AI-to-AI treaties directly, with no consent step, but **only through the original's gates**, per the report's §1a:
     - **Busy.** An AI is busy when it is at war, its mobilization is over 40, or it is winter. A busy AI declares no war and makes no alliance.
     - **War.** The target is the not-protected neighbour with the best power ratio `8·P(me)/max(1,P(k))`, starting from 10, with `P = (wealth/20000)·(unity/100)`, declared on `Random(10) == 0`.
     - **Alliance.** Only when not busy and `Random(20) == 0`. The partner is the first AI nation `m` already at war with one of the AI's neighbours `j`, where `m` has fewer than 2 wars and `cities[j] < cities[me] + cities[m]`. The setter's cascade then puts the AI at war with `j`.
     - **Trade.** Up to 3 partners on each side, including the swap of a poorer partner for a richer one.
     - **Partner.** Every trade and alliance write still requires a computer partner.
     - **Both rolls from one stream.** `Random(20)` draws from the seat-turn's `IRng`, the same stream as `Random(10)`, so a re-evaluated pass sees the same draw. No second generator is seeded from `state.RandomSeed`.
     - **Tests.** Each gate has a test that fails if it is deleted. One test runs a round of the classical scenario from uniform peace and shows **no alliance cascade**. Once T75 has merged, the classical game opens with the DAT's 5 wars, 13 trades and 4 alliances, so this test builds its state from the classical world with the starting relations replaced by uniform peace (flagged by T75's review, PR #367). The user saw about 38 alliances in round one; the original's rule makes an alliance only against a common enemy, so from uniform peace it forms none. Constants are ruleset data, cited to the report's §1a.
  4. **No war on an ally.** The AI's war targeting excludes any ally that holds a city. Its units attack only nations it is already at war with, so an attack is never an implicit declaration. A test gives an AI an allied, city-holding neighbour next to an attackable army and shows no declaration and no attack. The T65 #256 fixture's "declares war on its new ally" sequence no longer happens; update that expectation only if it is in this task's Owns, and otherwise report it.
  5. **Seeded outcomes.** Every seeded AI test, soak log or golden that changes is listed in the PR with its reason. The 50-seed soak keeps 0 rejected commands and its stall invariants.
  6. `dotnet build IC2.sln` and `dotnet test IC2.sln` are green, and the diff lists only Owns paths.
- **Hazards**:
  - **The neighbour mask.** The original reads nation field `+0x46` as a symmetric neighbour mask. The engine can derive "borders" from the world, but say how, and show it agrees with the report on the three saves it checked. **The user's decision of 2026-09-25 (PR #377's review, N1):** the geometric derivation ships as `[designed]`. It finds all nine confirmed pairs and adds three the original lacks (Rome–Greece, Thracia–Bithynia, Thracia–Seleucid); the remark and the PR name all three, and a test pins the checked rows exactly. A research pass checks whether the DAT carries the mask; if it does, a later task exports it into the world file and replaces the derivation.
  - **Don't break the human-to-human hotseat case.** "Always accepted" applies there, where the target is human.
  - **The toy world's AI may stop declaring war.** With `P = (wealth/20000)·(unity/100)`, the toy nations' wealth (hundreds) gives `P = 0`, so no toy nation passes the war gate. Check the report for whether the original computes P in integer arithmetic, and cite it. Report the 50-seed soak's count of wars, battles and sieges before and after. If combat vanishes from the toy soak, say so in the PR; the fix, if one is wanted, is toy-world data and a separate task.
  - **The soak may change a lot.** Fewer wars on allies and no auto-alliances with humans move AI outcomes. The toy soak has no human seat, so AI-to-AI should dominate there; confirm it.
