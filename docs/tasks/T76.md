- **Scope**: [`decompiled-mercenary-offer-list-and-position.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/main/docs/reports/decompiled-mercenary-offer-list-and-position.md) (research commit `aa629f3`):
  - **Every offer sits on a city tile.** The restock copies (x, y) from the DAT's 201-entry template table (`0x1FCD6`); a hire sets only troops to `0xFFFF`, so an empty slot keeps its position.
  - **The player's rule.** `TUnitMap_RecruitMercenaries` → `FUN_00449D08` takes the first live offer, in slot order, at Chebyshev distance **exactly 1** from the army, and uses its city. With none, the order does nothing. `TRecruitMercs_InitializeForm` then lists only the live offers on that city's tile, at most 10. Other refusals: 20 units, over 100,000 troops, the city's owner at war with the hiring nation, supplies below 0.15 % of troops, the fleet full.
  - **The AI's rule.** `FUN_0044E41C`, computer nations only, hires **every** live offer on any city at Chebyshev distance **< 5**, if the nation is at war with someone, the army holds **more than 50** money, it is not at war with the city's owner, and the army has a free unit slot. It charges **nothing** and applies no 100,000-troop, supply or fleet check.
  - **Out of scope**: `FUN_0044E84C`, which steers AI armies toward offer cities within distance 19, because when its target survives later movement choices is still open. The restock is T56's.
- **Done when**:
  1. **The slot has a position.** `MercenaryPoolSlot` carries its city tile. A native save written before this task loads through a real migration, and a save round-trips. The import maps `MercenaryRecord.X/Y` onto it, and T21's declared-unmapped entry for them is removed.
  2. **The player's rule.** `HireMercenaryCommand` is rejected, with a named code, unless the army is at Chebyshev distance exactly 1 from the offer's tile. Tests: distance 0 (the army's own tile, if one exists), 1 and 2; two offer cities adjacent to one army, where only the first live offer's city, in slot order, is available; an offer on a city whose owner is at war with the hiring nation. Every existing refusal keeps its code.
  3. **The AI's rule.** A computer nation's turn hires every qualifying offer, exactly as the report states. Tests fail if any one condition is removed: the distance boundary (4 hires, 5 does not), "at war with someone", the money threshold (50 does not hire, 51 does), not at war with the owner, a free slot. One more test shows the AI hire takes no money and ignores the 100,000-troop cap. Every threshold and the radius come from the ruleset, each with a `_provenance` citing the report and a corpus id.
  4. **Presets.** `classical-faithful` has the original's rules. What `improved` does with the AI's free hire is **posed to the user in the PR, not decided**; until then `improved` keeps the faithful rule and says so in its `_provenance`.
  5. **Determinism.** If the AI step draws, every draw goes through `IRng`, and the PR states how many draws it adds per AI turn. A fixed-seed test reproduces.
  6. `dotnet build IC2.sln` and `dotnet test IC2.sln` are green, and the diff lists only Owns paths.
- **Hazards**:
  - **"Exactly 1", not "at most 1".** An army never occupies a city tile. The original's check is `== 1`, the same one as mobilization's receiving army.
  - **The AI's radius is 4, not 5.** Mobilization's AI radius is 5 (`d < 6`); the mercenary hire uses `d < 5`. Do not reuse the mobilization constant.
  - **The AI pays nothing.** The asymmetry is the original's. A cost check that "fixes" it is a different rule and fails Done-when 3.
  - **Every seeded AI test downstream may change**, because the AI now spends turns hiring. Name each test that changes and say why, as T57 did.
