#### T202 The Italy-before-Rome campaign: world, ruleset and scenario

- **Release / priority**: 0.7 series (release-plan §2.4 item 4); P1. Issue [900](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial_conquest_2/issues/900).
- **Start after**: T199 (the scenario states its own goal), T200 (reachable from New Game) and T201 (terrain authoring).

### Decisions (owner, 2026-10-21)

- **D3, date and length:** 500 BC to 460 BC, a 40-year reign at the usual two weeks per turn, with no scenario turn limit. The first answer was 500 to 264 BC; at two weeks per turn that is about 5,700 turns, so the owner chose the 40-year reign instead.
- **D4, nations:** twelve, all of them playable by a human: Rome, the Etruscans, Samnites, Umbrians, Veneti, Ligurians, Senones, Insubres, Campanians, Magna Graecia (led from Taras), Syracuse, and Carthage (western Sicily and Sardinia).
- **D5, map:** a portrait map from the Po valley to Sicily, with Corsica and Sardinia, and no city in Africa.
- **D6, barbarians:** later. The Gauls are ordinary nations, and #900 stays open until T203.

### Scope (after D3–D6)

1. **A new world**, `data/worlds/italy-<year>.json` plus its terrain sidecar from T201's importer, with cities, nations, starting relations and starting armies and fleets.
2. **A ruleset copy**, because rulesets have no inheritance. It keeps the classical rules and changes only the calendar years and the map-scale constants the campaign needs. Each change is marked `[designed]` with its reason.
3. **A scenario** with seats, the victory condition and goal, and `listed: true`.
4. **Provenance.** Every historical fact (a city, a date, a faction) cites a source under the existing provenance rules. Everything else is marked `[designed]`. No original-game data or art is used.

### Owns

New files only: `data/worlds/italy-*.json` and its sidecar, `data/rulesets/italy-*.json`, `data/scenarios/italy-*.json`, the source PNG under `tools/` or `assets/` (our own art), and tests that load and resolve it.

### Notes from the work

- One script, `tools/Italy/build_italy.py`, holds the tables: the coastline, relief, lake and river shapes, the twelve nations, the 231 towns and the starting forces. It writes the terrain PNG and the three JSON files; `tools/TerrainImport` then writes the sidecar from the PNG.
- Owner review (2026-10-21), round 1, asked for more forest, hills, rivers and lakes, and more towns. The map gained 38 rivers, eight lakes (Lake Fucinus among them) and 231 towns, each with its source.
- Round 2 refined it:
  - **No hills tile.** As in the original, forest stands for wooded hill country.
  - **Clipped frame.** The map stops at the Alpine crest; the Balkan shore and Africa are drawn as sea, so no empty country is left.
  - **Passable Apennines.** They are mostly forest (cost 2), with mountains only at the high massifs: Gran Sasso, the Majella, the Sibillini, Pollino, Etna and others.
  - **Plains stay plain.**
  - **Size.** 139 × 155 tiles of about 7 km, 6,373 of them land. The classical map's tiles are about 20 km, so Italy gets about four times the room it has on the classical map.
- The script also sets river tiles to the connectivity codes the renderer reads: 6 W–E, 7 N–S, 8 E+N, 9 E+S, 10 W+S, 11 W+N.
- `docs/scenario-authoring.md` gains the `historical:` provenance tag.
- `GameSession`'s end-of-reign text took its years from a hard-coded 20. It now computes them from the ruleset: still 20 under the classical rules, 40 here.

### Done when

1. The world, ruleset and scenario resolve, pass cross-document validation (T199) and appear in New Game (T200).
2. A seeded all-AI game runs 200 seat-turns with no exception, no rejected command and no full-round stall (#1290's rule).
3. Every non-designed fact has a citation in the data's `_provenance`, checked by a test.
