- **Scope**:
  - **Protects**: an AI army at war chooses its action by the original's target tree, in both presets (the user's decisions of 2026-10-09). When the tree selects *attack the city* or *attack the army*, the AI issues that attack (or the march toward it). **An army that did not move takes the garrison fallback**, so no army stays idle with moves left after its chosen action failed. #907's case, Gaul's `army-9` beside Pisae, is the reference: under the tree, its city score clears 100 (the clone's siege strength 35,640 against Pisae's defence 32,950 gives `35,640 × 110 / 32,950 = 118` with integer division, minus distance 1, then doubled because the defence is below the strength and the city is within 7). The exact value depends on the original's `FUN_0044a930` strength, which T156 implements.
  - **The rule** ([`2026-10-07-strategic-ai-turn.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/main/docs/reports/2026-10-07-strategic-ai-turn.md) §3.3 and §5, **[confirmed: decompile]**; the in-play corrections of [`2026-10-09-ai-intercept-and-fleet-hunt-in-play.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/main/docs/reports/2026-10-09-ai-intercept-and-fleet-hunt-in-play.md) are a Wine candidate):
    - **The resupply/defence city** (`FUN_0044e670`): an own city whose supply stock is below the army's strength (`troops/100`), or, only while at war, a foreign non-war city with stock above `strength + 80` and `money > strength/5`. Own capitals score −20 and foreign cities +20. If nothing qualifies, or the best foreign city is beyond 15, the nearest own city.
    - **The best enemy city** (`FUN_0044ece4`): `score = strength×110 / cityDefense − distance`, then:
      - halved if the city is in another region;
      - doubled if `cityDefense < strength` and `distance < 7`;
      - doubled if the city is a capital and `cityDefense×⅔ < strength`;
      - returned as `(city, score + distance, distance)`.
    - **The best enemy field army** (`FUN_0044ee60`): `strength×110 / theirStrength − distance`, halved across regions, capped at 1000, with **+1000** when the enemy is weaker and within 7.
    - **The decision**, verbatim, with `moves` the army's remaining moves:
      ```text
      if (armyScore < 100
          or (supplies < 1 and morale < 60 and armyDist > 8)
          or (cityScore > 100 and cityDist < moves and armyDist > 2×moves)):
          if (cityScore < 100 or (supplies < 1 and cityDist > 19)):
              if (troops/500 < supplies and a city target exists):
                  the mercenary run (FUN_0044e84c), then defend the resupply city
                  (armyScore < 71 and cityScore > 85) or still chase the army target (armyScore ≥ 71)
              else: move to the resupply/defence city
          else: attack the city
      else: attack the army target
      if the army did not move: the garrison fallback (FUN_0044ebe8)
      ```
      The boundaries are exactly as written: `< 100` and `> 100` differ at 100.
  - **`improved`** uses the same tree for these decisions, and keeps its designed extras elsewhere (personalities, fleets, diplomacy, its economy).
  - **Out of scope**: the economy phase ([T157](T157.md)); the fleet phase; the turn's fixed phase order and the action cap (the designed loop stays the driver: only each army's choice changes, plus the post-execution fallback); the battle itself.
- **Done when**:
  1. **The scorers are the original's.** Unit tests pin each scorer on hand-built states: the city score's region halving, its two doublings and `+ distance` on return; the army score's 1000 cap and its +1000; the resupply city's −20 and +20, and the beyond-15 fallback. Each test fails when its term is removed (show the mutation).
  2. **The decision tree is the original's.** A table-driven test walks every branch of the decision above: attack the army, attack the city, the mercenary run with each of its two continuations, and move to the resupply city. Each boundary has a case on each side (100 for both `<` and `>`, 85, 71, 8, 19, `2×moves`, supplies 1, morale 60), and each fails if its threshold or comparison changes.
  3. **The fallback runs after execution, keyed to that army's tile.** Two tests, each failing if the fallback is removed:
     - an army whose chosen move is accepted but moves nothing (a path blocked by a city, as in #907);
     - an army whose chosen command changes something else (its supply, a treasury) but not its tile.

     In both, the garrison fallback runs that turn for that army. A third test shows that an army that did move takes no fallback.
  4. **#907's reproduction no longer parks** (amended by the user's decision of 2026-10-10, on #925). `classical-mediterranean`, seat Rome, the default seed: Gaul's `army-9`, once beside Pisae, attacks within 3 turns, in both presets, a target the tree selects (Pisae, or the Roman field army the tree scores higher there); it never stands beside Pisae with moves left. Under today's designed gate it never attacks, so this fails before the change. Which target it picks is the tree's: the original's region boxes (`FUN_0044eb18`), which might make it Pisae, are not transcribed yet (a research follow-up), so `cityRegionById` stays empty.
  5. **An army attack is carried out.** A fixed hand-built state in which the tree selects *attack the army* (a weaker enemy field army within 7, at war): the AI issues the attack, or the march toward it, and the engine accepts it. The test fails if the army-attack branch's command is removed.
  6. **Attack decisions are carried out.** Instrumented by the tree's own decision log, over 16 rounds on the default seed, seeds 7 and 123 as Rome, and the default seed as Carthage: whenever the tree selected *attack the city* or *attack the army* for an army, an attack or a march toward that target was issued and accepted that turn. A target the existing commands cannot reach is never selected (the reachability rule under Hazards), and the PR states how many candidates it skipped in these runs. A count of zero exceptions is asserted, and the PR states the counts of attack decisions and of AI captures for these runs, before the change and after it.
  7. **Nothing regresses.** The 50-seed soak reports 0 rejected and 0 probe mismatches within its budget. The changed goldens and seeded expectations are regenerated or updated and listed with their reason. `dotnet build` and `dotnet test` are green, and the diff lists only Owns paths.
- **Hazards**:
  - **Every seeded game changes.** The AI attacks where it used to wait, so list every changed golden, soak line and seeded test.
  - **Movement is unchanged.** The original's destination call walks and attacks as it goes, routing around obstacles or shipping the army; the clone keeps its own move and attack commands, whose walker follows a straight line and stops at a city, an army, a fleet or a sea tile. The tree chooses the target, and the existing commands carry it out. **The reachability rule** (the user's decision of 2026-10-10, on #925; `[designed]`, the same idea as #907's fix): a target whose straight-line walk is stopped before it reaches the target is skipped, and the tree takes its next-best target of the same kind, then the next branch, as if the skipped one were absent. A test shows a sea-separated and a blocked target are skipped and the next-best is chosen. Routing around obstacles and shipping armies, as the original does, is a later task, not this one; do not add a mover.
  - **Strength.** The scorers use the original's `FUN_0044a930` strength (§5). If it differs from the clone's existing siege strength, implement §5's and say where the two differ.
