- **Scope**: Research [`decompiled-quarterly-rebellion.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/main/docs/reports/decompiled-quarterly-rebellion.md) (research `235af11`, **[confirmed: decompile + listing]**) is the source for every rule below. The quarterly city loop (`FUN_00451B40`) calls `FUN_0044C204` for every **non-capital city with loyalty below 30**. A city counts as a capital if it is the capital of any nation, dead ones included. The routine itself draws no `Random` and changes nothing but the owner. In code order:
  1. **The owner is not the city's allegiance nation.**
     - **(a) Rebirth.** If the allegiance nation's unity is 0 or below, the rebirth check runs, and nothing else happens: if rebirth declines, the city stays put. Rebirth itself is **T87**'s. Here, this branch does nothing but leave a place, and a remark, for T87's call.
     - **(b) Allegiance.** Otherwise the city defects to its allegiance nation. There is no other condition: no war, no distance, and the allegiance nation may be human.
  2. **The owner is the city's allegiance nation.**
     - **(c) An attacker.** Scan every army in list order, without breaking. An army matches if it is live, its Chebyshev distance to the city is **below 10** (so 9 or less), and its owner is at war with the city's owner. The city goes to the owner of the **last** matching army. Troops, morale and embarkation are not read.
     - **(d) A neighbour.** If no army matched, a nation `n` is a candidate if it neighbours the owner (T86's neighbour set in the game state) and its unity is above 0. Its score is `cities(n) − 2 × Chebyshev(city, capital(n))`. The best score starts at −1000, and only a strictly greater score replaces it, so the **lowest nation index wins a tie**. There is no relation test: an ally, or a human, may receive the city.
     - **Otherwise** nothing happens: no write, no news.
  - **The transfer** is the original's defection (`FUN_0044BED8`), whose news line is "*C defects from X to Y.*". The news comes before the new season's header. The engine's `CityCaptureResolver.Defect`, as corrected by T86, is that defection.
  - **The loop's draws** (report §4): per city, `Random(4)` if the tax rate is 10 or less and loyalty is below 80; then `Random(3)`; then `Random(taxRate)` if that draw returned 0. The rebellion check comes after these. The original advances the seed even for `Random(0)`, but the engine's `CityLoyaltyDraws` skips the draw when the tax rate is 0, which is a real divergence of the random stream. This task corrects it.
- **Done when**:
  1. **The research is stated.** The PR's first section gives the rule above with the report's citations, and each code remark carries the report's tag.
  2. **The trigger.** The rebellion runs in the quarterly city loop, after that city's loyalty draws, for a non-capital city at loyalty 29 but not 30. A capital at 29, including a dead nation's capital, doesn't rebel.
  3. **Each branch, at its conditions.** Each has a test that fails when the branch is removed:
     - (a): a dead allegiance nation, where the city stays and there's no fallback to (c) or (d).
     - (b): a live allegiance nation, including a human one, at peace.
     - (c): an army at Chebyshev distance 9 and at 10; a live army not at war; and two matching armies, where the last one wins.
     - (d): a neighbour at unity 0; an ally as the receiver; a tie, where the lowest index wins; and the no-candidate case, where nothing happens.
  4. **The transfer is a defection.** It goes through `CityCaptureResolver.Defect`, with the news line and the defection's loyalty, unity and treasury effects. A test shows the receiver's treasury gain and the old owner's unity floor.
  5. **The tax-0 draw.** `CityLoyaltyDraws` advances the stream for `Random(taxRate)` even when the tax rate is 0, in the report's order. A seeded test pins a quarter at tax 0, and another reproduces a quarter with a rebellion exactly.
  6. **Seeded outcomes.** Rebel cities now change hands in long games. Every seeded test, measurement, soak or golden that changes is listed with its reason. The 50-seed soak keeps 0 rejected commands.
  7. `dotnet build IC2.sln` and `dotnet test IC2.sln` green, and the diff lists only Owns paths.
- **Hazards**:
  - **Army order.** (c)'s tie-break follows the army list. The original's list is in creation order, except that compaction moves its last record into each hole. The engine's list order is its own. Say in the remark how the engine's order relates to the original's, and test the tie on the engine's order.
  - **Live reads.** A rebellion earlier in the quarter changes later cities' scores, because counts and unity are read live. Read them live too; don't take a snapshot at the start of the quarter.
  - **A rebellion that eliminates a nation.** The original's defection path handles that case, and T86 corrects the engine's version of it. Reuse the path; don't write a second one.
  - **The rebirth branch.** T87 adds the call. Here the branch must exist and do nothing, with a remark naming T87.
  - **Many outcomes move.** Rebellions change long games everywhere. List them rather than tuning anything.
