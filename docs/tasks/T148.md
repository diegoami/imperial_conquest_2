#### T148 The map's surface is painted, not tiled: blended terrain, irregular coasts, river strokes

- **Design milestone**: M18 (the Godot UI). A correction task for bug [#789](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial_conquest_2/issues/789), from the user's play on 2026-10-06: *"the terrain tiles look completely lifeless, compared to the original; they need to be kind of decent."* The user put it in the v0.5.0 gate the same day ([build-process.md §4.8](../build-process.md#48-the-playability-gate-until-v050)). **Rescoped on 2026-10-06 by the user's decision**, after the first attempt (PR #806: per-cell tiles with hashed variants) read as a checkerboard: *"worth thinking of an algorithm to make the tile surface look more organic, with gradual change, and less chaotic / checkered"*. The user chose **texture splatting**: one seamless texture per terrain, painted continuously across the map and blended per pixel, so the surface has no cell grid, terrains fade into each other, and coasts are irregular. - **Scope**:
  - **Protects**: the world data and the engine's rules (no change under `src/IC2.Engine/` outside `Assets/AssetKeys.cs`, nothing under `data/**`); the terrain grid's meaning and `_terrainCells` (the click path, MapClickCheck: a click still selects the cell under the pointer); the drawn terrain rectangle, `W × H × BaseTileSize × zoom` at `_pan` (MapClipCheck); T147's fit, clamp and centring; the markers, their keys, tints and order; the Area map (`godot/UI/AreaMapView.cs`), which keeps its flat colours; the inspector `godot/MapViewer.cs`; every CLI golden. Forbidden: a random draw (`IRng` or `System.Random`) for anything in the surface; an original game file, or anything derived from one, in a prompt, a pack or the repository.
  - **The surface keys** **[designed: this task]**: `terrain.plain.surface`, `terrain.desert.surface`, `terrain.forest.surface`, `terrain.mountain.surface`, `terrain.sea_shallow.surface` and `terrain.sea_deep.surface`. Each is one **seamless** square texture, 256 × 256, 24-bit BMP (asset-specification.md §1.2), drawn top-down, which tiles in both directions without a visible seam.
  - **The splat map** **[designed]**. `TerrainSplatMap` turns the world's terrain grid into weights for the six surfaces at a resolution of **4 samples per cell** (1,280 × 560 on the classical world), which the shader samples with linear filtering:
    - **Classes.** Plain, desert, forest and mountains are their own class. A river cell (codes 6–11) counts as **plain** for the surface (its river is a stroke, below). A sea cell (codes 0–1) is **shallow** when a land cell lies within 2 cells of it, else **deep**, whatever its code, because the shipped world has no code-1 cell (the first attempt's report, confirmed by the Opus review's R9 note).
    - **Blending.** Each sample's weights come from the classes of the cells around it, smoothed so a terrain fades into its neighbour over about **half a cell** on each side of the shared edge, and the sampling position is displaced by a fixed, deterministic 2-D value noise (amplitude at most **0.3 cell**, a few octaves) so borders and coasts are irregular rather than straight. The noise is a pure function of position and a constant seed, so the surface is identical on every run and in every save.
    - **The cell's own terrain wins at its centre.** At every cell's centre sample, the cell's own class has the highest weight. A city, an army or a click target therefore always sits on its own terrain, and the displacement never moves a coastline across a cell centre.
    - **Shore.** Where the land and sea weights are both above a threshold, the shader adds a narrow band of sand toward the land and surf toward the sea, so coasts read without shore tiles.
  - **The draw** **[designed]**. The shader draws the whole terrain rectangle in one pass at every zoom. It samples each surface texture in **world space**, with one texture repeat spanning a few cells (the implementer picks and names the scale), and mipmaps keep the far zoom calm. The flat-colour texture stays as the fallback when the shader or a texture is missing, and for the Area map. Memory: the splat map plus the six textures and their mipmaps stay under **16 MB** (`TerrainMemoryBytesForCheck`, counted as width × height × 4 for every texture and image the draw holds, mip levels included).
  - **Rivers** **[confirmed: code, `godot/MapViewer.cs` `DrawRiver` (~:161–169) and the world's tile types `river_1`…`river_6`]**: a river cell's connectivity code gives its exits (6 east–west, 7 north–south, 8 east+north, 9 east+south, 10 west+south, 11 west+north). `TerrainSplatMap` turns each river cell into a stroke from exit midpoint to exit midpoint, curved through the cell rather than bent at its centre **[designed]**. Strokes of neighbouring cells meet at the shared edge's midpoint, so a river is one continuous line. `GameMapView` draws them over the surface in a water colour, about a fifth of a cell wide with a minimum on-screen width, once a cell is drawn at 4 px or more.
  - **The art** **[designed, a default the user settles at the visual review]**. The six textures are generated through `scripts/generate-authored-assets.ps1` with a new `surface` kind. The prompt asks for a seamless top-down texture, and the conform step makes it tile regardless: it offsets the image by half its size and cross-fades the seam, then resizes to 256 × 256. The palette follows asset-specification.md §4.5: the plain's bright green, the forest's darker green with tree texture, the mountain's grey rock with snow flecks (top-down, never a landscape with a sky), the desert's olive-to-tan sand, and lighter shallow and darker deep water with a gentle wave texture. The prompts carry words only, never an original image. The placeholder pack gets procedural seamless stand-ins. Generation spends the owner's OpenRouter credit: generate each key once, regenerate only when the result fails conformance or the visual review, and list in the PR what was generated and how often.
  - **Not this task's**: the Area map's colours; city, army and fleet art; animated water; the season overlay (bug #339); a terrain legend (bug #722).

- **Hazards**:
  - **Two sources of truth.** The weights are computed in C# and only sampled by the shader, so the tested code is the drawn code. If the shader needs maths of its own (the shore band, the world-space sampling), keep it small, and keep it out of anything Done-when 1 asserts.
  - **The centre rule and the noise.** Too much displacement moves a coastline across a cell centre, so a port appears inland or a ship on land. Done-when 1 pins the centre rule; lower the amplitude rather than relax it.
  - **Generated textures may not tile.** The conform step's offset-and-cross-fade guarantees the seam; the visual review settles the look. A texture the user rejects is regenerated in a rework round, never hand-painted.
  - **The key and the cost.** The OpenRouter key is the owner's (`OPENROUTER_API_KEY` or `assets-generator.local.ini`); never print, log or commit it. Never put an original screenshot or tile into a prompt.
