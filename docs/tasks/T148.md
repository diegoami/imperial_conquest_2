#### T148 The map's surface is painted, not tiled: blended terrain, irregular coasts, river strokes

- **Design milestone**: M18 (the Godot UI). A correction task for bug [#789](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial_conquest_2/issues/789), from the user's play on 2026-10-06: *"the terrain tiles look completely lifeless, compared to the original; they need to be kind of decent."* The user put it in the v0.5.0 gate the same day ([build-process.md §4.8](../build-process.md#48-the-playability-gate-until-v050)). **Rescoped on 2026-10-06 by the user's decision**, after the first attempt (PR #806: per-cell tiles with hashed variants) read as a checkerboard: *"worth thinking of an algorithm to make the tile surface look more organic, with gradual change, and less chaotic / checkered"*. The user chose **texture splatting**: one seamless texture per terrain, painted continuously across the map and blended per pixel, so the surface has no cell grid, terrains fade into each other, and coasts are irregular. The rework continues PR #806's branch; the Opus review of that attempt ([PR #806](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial_conquest_2/pull/806#issuecomment-6015899659), R1–R11) is answered by this design (R1–R5 and R10–R11 no longer apply; R6–R8 are closed by the Owns below). **Labels**: `phase:3 lane:ui release:v0.5.0 local-only` (the textures are generated with the owner's OpenRouter key on this machine).

- **Scope**:
  - **Protects**: the world data and the engine's rules (no change under `src/IC2.Engine/` outside `Assets/AssetKeys.cs`, nothing under `data/**`); the terrain grid's meaning and `_terrainCells` (the click path, MapClickCheck: a click still selects the cell under the pointer); the drawn terrain rectangle, `W × H × BaseTileSize × zoom` at `_pan` (MapClipCheck); T147's fit, clamp and centring; the markers, their keys, tints and order; the Area map (`godot/UI/AreaMapView.cs`), which keeps its flat colours; the inspector `godot/MapViewer.cs`; every CLI golden. Forbidden: a random draw (`IRng` or `System.Random`) for anything in the surface; an original game file, or anything derived from one, in a prompt, a pack or the repository.
  - **The surface keys** **[designed: this task]**: `terrain.plain.surface`, `terrain.desert.surface`, `terrain.forest.surface`, `terrain.mountain.surface`, `terrain.sea_shallow.surface` and `terrain.sea_deep.surface`. Each is one **seamless** square texture, 256 × 256, 24-bit BMP (asset-specification.md §1.2), drawn top-down, which tiles in both directions without a visible seam.
  - **The splat map** **[designed]**. `TerrainSplatMap` turns the world's terrain grid into weights for the six surfaces. It exposes the **continuous function** `WeightsAt(x, y)` (map coordinates in cells, a cell spanning [x, x + 1) × [y, y + 1), so its centre is (x + 0.5, y + 0.5)), and bakes it on a **lattice of 4 × 4 samples per cell** (1,280 × 560 on the classical world), each sample taken at the centre of its quarter-cell, (x + (i + 0.5) / 4, y + (j + 0.5) / 4). The shader samples the bake with linear filtering, so at a cell's centre it reads the mean of that cell's four inner samples (Sol's review of PR 807, R1). **The representation** (R3): the six weights go to the shader as **two RGBA8 textures** of the lattice's size, `splat_a` holding plain, desert, forest and mountain in R, G, B and A, and `splat_b` holding shallow and deep sea in R and G (B and A unused), the six weights of a sample summing to 255 ± 2:
    - **Classes.** Plain, desert, forest and mountains are their own class. A river cell (codes 6–11) counts as **plain** for the surface (its river is a stroke, below). A sea cell (codes 0–1) is **shallow** when a land cell lies within **1 cell** of it (Chebyshev; the user's decisions of 2026-10-06 at the visual review of the splatting round: a thin rim, not a 2-cell band), else **deep**, whatever its code, because the shipped world has no code-1 cell (the first attempt's report, confirmed by the Opus review's R9 note).
    - **Blending.** Each sample's weights come from the classes of the cells around it, smoothed so a terrain fades into its neighbour over about **a quarter of a cell** on each side of the shared edge (crisp, not soft; the user's decisions of 2026-10-06 at the visual review of the splatting round, *"too blurry"*), and the sampling position is displaced by a fixed, deterministic 2-D value noise so borders and coasts are irregular rather than straight: it includes a **low-frequency** octave with a period of 3 to 4 cells, so a diagonal coast becomes a wavy line rather than a cell staircase, and its magnitude is at most **0.45 cell** (the user's decisions of 2026-10-06 at the visual review of the splatting round). The centre rule below still binds: lower the amplitude rather than break it. The noise is a pure function of position and a constant seed, so the surface is identical on every run and in every save.
    - **The cell's own terrain wins at its centre.** At every cell's centre, the cell's own class has the highest weight, both in `WeightsAt` at (x + 0.5, y + 0.5) and in the mean of the cell's four inner baked samples, which is what the linear-filtered shader reads there. A city, an army or a click target therefore always sits on its own terrain, and the displacement never moves a coastline across a cell centre.
    - **Shore.** Where the land and sea weights are both above a threshold, the shader adds a narrow band of sand toward the land and surf toward the sea, so coasts read without shore tiles.
  - **The draw** **[designed]**. The shader draws the whole terrain rectangle in one pass at every zoom. It samples each surface texture in **world space**, with one texture repeat spanning **2 to 3 cells** (the implementer picks and names the scale; the user's decisions of 2026-10-06 at the visual review of the splatting round, *"too blurry"*), with **repeat enabled** on the six surface samplers (`repeat_enable` and a mipmapped linear filter hint) and **repeat disabled** on the two splat samplers: without it, every terrain beyond the first repeat is its texture's clamped edge pixel, the cause the Opus review of the splatting round found (R1). Mipmaps keep the far zoom calm. The shallow sea and the surf band are **toned down**, a light rim rather than bright cyan (the user's decisions of 2026-10-06 at the visual review of the splatting round). The flat-colour texture stays as the fallback when the shader or a texture is missing, and for the Area map. Memory: everything the terrain draw holds alive stays under **24 MB** (`TerrainMemoryBytesForCheck`; Sol's review of PR 807, R3): width × height × 4 bytes for every `Image` and every `Texture2D` it retains, counted separately (an `Image` kept after its texture is made counts too), a mipmapped one counted with its whole chain (× 4/3), and the flat-colour fallback included. On the classical world the two splat textures are about 5.7 MB and the six mipmapped surfaces about 2.1 MB, so dropping the source `Image`s after upload keeps it well inside the bound.
  - **Rivers** **[confirmed: code, `godot/MapViewer.cs` `DrawRiver` (~:161–169) and the world's tile types `river_1`…`river_6`]**: a river cell's connectivity code gives its exits (6 east–west, 7 north–south, 8 east+north, 9 east+south, 10 west+south, 11 west+north). `TerrainSplatMap` follows each river from cell to cell by those exits and fits **one smooth curve** through the chain (a Catmull-Rom or equivalent spline through the river cells' centres, with a small deterministic wobble of at most 0.15 cell), so a river meanders instead of running along cell mid-lines with quarter-arc corners **[designed: the user's decisions of 2026-10-06 at the visual review of the splatting round]**. The curve passes within 0.2 cell of every river cell's centre and is continuous with a continuous tangent along the whole river; where the codes break the chain (a confluence, or an exit into the sea or the map's edge), the curve ends at that cell's exit midpoint. `GameMapView` draws them over the surface in a water colour, about a fifth of a cell wide with a minimum on-screen width, once a cell is drawn at 4 px or more.
  - **The art** **[designed, a default the user settles at the visual review]**. The six textures are generated through `scripts/generate-authored-assets.ps1` with a new `surface` kind. The prompt asks for a seamless top-down texture, and the conform step makes it tile regardless: it offsets the image by half its size and cross-fades the resulting central cross with a mask that leaves no visible "+" seam, then resizes to 256 × 256. It adds **no edge fade or mirroring** toward the opposite edge (the Opus review of the splatting round, R4: that fade mirrored the textures near every repeat border). The palette follows asset-specification.md §4.5: the plain's bright green, the forest's darker green with tree texture, the mountain's grey rock with snow flecks (top-down, never a landscape with a sky), the desert's olive-to-tan sand, and lighter shallow and darker deep water with a gentle wave texture. The prompts carry words only, never an original image. The placeholder pack gets procedural seamless stand-ins. Generation spends the owner's OpenRouter credit: generate each key once, regenerate only when the result fails conformance or the visual review, and list in the PR what was generated and how often.
  - **Not this task's**: the Area map's colours; city, army and fleet art; animated water; the season overlay (bug #339); a terrain legend (bug #722).

- **Hazards**:
  - **Two sources of truth.** The weights are computed in C# and only sampled by the shader, so the tested code is the drawn code. If the shader needs maths of its own (the shore band, the world-space sampling), keep it small, and keep it out of anything Done-when 1 asserts.
  - **The centre rule and the noise.** Too much displacement moves a coastline across a cell centre, so a port appears inland or a ship on land. Done-when 1 pins the centre rule; lower the amplitude rather than relax it.
  - **Generated textures may not tile.** The conform step's offset-and-cross-fade guarantees the seam; the visual review settles the look. A texture the user rejects is regenerated in a rework round, never hand-painted.
  - **The key and the cost.** The OpenRouter key is the owner's (`OPENROUTER_API_KEY` or `assets-generator.local.ini`); never print, log or commit it. Never put an original screenshot or tile into a prompt.
