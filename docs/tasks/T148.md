#### T148 The map draws terrain from tiles: varied land and sea, joined rivers, shores

- **Design milestone**: M18 (the Godot UI). A correction task for bug [#789](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial_conquest_2/issues/789), from the user's play on 2026-10-06: *"the terrain tiles look completely lifeless, compared to the original; they need to be kind of decent."* The user put it in the v0.5.0 gate the same day ([build-process.md §4.8](../build-process.md#48-the-playability-gate-until-v050): *"An item the user calls a blocker is `release:v0.5.0`"*). The user chose the scope: draw the map from per-cell tiles, and regenerate better tiles with T51's generator, with variation per tile type, shore edges, and the six river pieces. Today `GameMapView.BakeTerrainTexture` (~:340–371) bakes **one pixel per cell** from `TerrainColorsByName` (~:83–92) and stretches it, so every cell is a flat colour, and a river cell is a blue square with no course. The packs already carry seven 32 × 32 `terrain.*.tile` sprites that the game map never draws (T48 drew only the markers). **Labels**: `phase:3 lane:ui release:v0.5.0 local-only` (the art is generated with the owner's OpenRouter key on this machine).

- **Scope**:
  - **Protects**: the world data and the engine's rules (no change under `src/IC2.Engine/` outside `Assets/AssetKeys.cs`, and nothing under `data/**`); the terrain grid's meaning and `_terrainCells` (the click path, MapClickCheck); the drawn terrain rectangle, `W × H × BaseTileSize × zoom` at `_pan` (MapClipCheck); the markers, their keys, tints and order; the Area map (`godot/UI/AreaMapView.cs`), which keeps its flat colours; the inspector `godot/MapViewer.cs`; every CLI golden. Forbidden: a random draw (`IRng` or `System.Random`) for the variants; an original game file, or anything derived from one, in a prompt, a pack or the repository; a change to any marker asset.
  - **The new keys** **[designed: this task; asset-specification.md §4.5 recorded the river gap]**:
    - **Variants**: `terrain.<type>.tile.2`, `.3` and `.4` for plain, desert, forest, mountain, sea_coastal and sea_deep (the existing `terrain.<type>.tile` is variant 1): 18 keys;
    - **Rivers**: `terrain.river.ew`, `.ns`, `.en`, `.es`, `.ws` and `.wn`, one per river code 6–11 in that order, the connectivity `godot/MapViewer.cs`'s `DrawRiver` (~:161–169) already reads from the codes (6 east–west, 7 north–south, 8 east+north, 9 east+south, 10 west+south, 11 west+north) **[confirmed: code, `MapViewer.DrawRiver`, and the world's tile types `river_1`…`river_6`]**. A river tile is land with the river's course across it; its ends meet the cell's edge midpoints, so neighbouring pieces join. `terrain.river.tile` stays as the fallback;
    - **Shores**: `terrain.shore.n`, `.e`, `.s` and `.w`, 32-bit overlays with transparency, drawn over a **land** cell (codes 2–11) on each side whose neighbour is sea (codes 0–1): a band of sand and surf along that edge. A cell beyond the map's edge counts as not sea.
  - **The variant choice** **[designed]**: deterministic from the cell's position, `variant = (x × 73856093 ^ y × 19349663) mod 4 + 1` on non-negative integers (or another fixed hash the PR names), so the map looks the same on every run and in every save, and no draw is spent. A key the pack lacks falls back to variant 1, then to the flat colour.
  - **The draw** **[designed]**: when a cell is drawn at **12 px or more** on screen, the terrain is drawn from the tiles; below that, the flat-colour texture is drawn as today (tiles shrunk under 12 px read as noise). The tiles are drawn either per visible cell, culled to the control's rectangle, or from chunked bakes; the implementer chooses and names it in the PR. Two limits hold: no single texture over 4096 px a side, and the terrain textures together under **64 MB** (a whole-map bake at 32 px a cell would be about 183 MB). The drawn rectangle is exactly today's, so MapClipCheck's contract holds. Filtering stays `Nearest` (the user's Q-B choice, ~:166–171).
  - **The art** **[designed, a default the user settles at the visual review]**: every authored tile is regenerated through `scripts/generate-authored-assets.ps1`, styled after the original's look as asset-specification.md §4.5 samples it: the plain's bright green with a darker ridge line and speckles, the mountain's grey peaks with snow caps over a red-brown foothill band, the desert in the olive-to-tan range, the two seas' wave motif (lighter coastal, darker deep). The four variants of a type differ in detail, not colour, so a field of them reads as one terrain without a repeating grid. The prompts carry words only, never an original image. The placeholder pack gets procedural stand-ins for every new key, so every key resolves in both packs. Generation spends OpenRouter credit on the owner's key, so the implementer generates once per key, and regenerates a key only when the earlier result fails conformance; the PR lists the keys generated and how often.
  - **Not this task's**: the Area map's colours; city, army and fleet art; animated water; the season overlay (bug #339); a terrain legend (bug #722).
- **Done when**:
  1. **The keys.** `TerrainTileKeysTests`: every tile code 0–11 maps to its key (rivers to their connectivity piece, 6 → `terrain.river.ew` … 11 → `terrain.river.wn`); a land cell with sea to its north and west gets `terrain.shore.n` and `.w` and no others, a sea cell gets none, and a cell on the map's edge gets none on that side; the variant of a cell is the same on two calls and lies in 1–4, and over the classical world's 320 × 140 cells each variant of plain takes between 15% and 35% of the plain cells. `AssetKeysTests` lists the 28 new keys.
  2. **The packs.** `AuthoredPackConformanceTests` and `AssetValidationAgainstPlaceholderPackTests`: both packs resolve every terrain key; each tile is a 32 × 32 BMP, 24-bit for a base, variant or river tile and 32-bit with transparency for a shore overlay (asset-specification.md §1.2–1.3), and each shore overlay's opaque pixels lie within 10 px of its named edge.
  3. **The draw.** `TerrainTilesCheck`, run with `godot --headless --path godot res://Checks/TerrainTilesCheck.tscn --quit-after 600`: at a zoom that draws a cell at 32 px, `TerrainKeysForCheck` reports, for a scripted set of cells (a plain, a forest, a deep sea, a river of code 8, a coastal land cell), the keys of Done-when 1; at exactly 12 px a cell it reports the tile path, and at 11.9 px the flat-colour path. **Memory**: `TerrainMemoryBytesForCheck` returns the sum, over every `Image` and every `Texture2D` the terrain draw holds alive at that moment (the decoded tiles, every variant, overlay and chunk, and the flat-colour texture), of width × height × 4 bytes each, an `Image` and the texture made from it counted separately; the check asserts it is under 64 MB (67,108,864 bytes) after drawing the whole map at 12 px and then at 72 px a cell. MapClipCheck, MapClickCheck, AreaMapCheck and AssetPackSelectionCheck exit 0 with 0 FAIL, unchanged. **The check proves itself** (Sol's review of PR 793, R5): with the tile threshold mutated above `MaxZoom` × `BaseTileSize`, uncommitted, the check fails on the 32 px and 12 px cases, and passes again once the mutation is reverted; the PR shows both runs. The C# tests of Done-when 1 and 2 are new and need no run on `main`.
  4. **For the visual review.** A short **windowed** run at 2560 × 1351 (headless screenshots do not work, #156) saves screenshots under the worktree's `rendered/`: the Mediterranean at the default fit, Italy and Greece at a 32 px cell, and the Nile or another river's course at a 32 px cell. The PR names them and sets them beside one of the original's map screenshots the user names; the user reviews them.
  5. **Nothing else moves.** `git diff origin/main -- data src/IC2.Engine tests/fixtures` shows only `AssetKeys.cs`; every CLI golden is byte-identical.
  6. `dotnet build IC2.sln --nologo -v q`, `dotnet test IC2.sln --nologo -v q` and `dotnet build godot/IC2.MapViewer.csproj` are green, and `git diff --name-only origin/main...HEAD` lists only Owns paths.
- **Hazards**:
  - **Memory.** A whole-map texture at tile resolution is about 183 MB and over Godot's texture size limit. Done-when 3 bounds it; a draw that bakes the whole map at tile resolution is out.
  - **Frame time.** Drawing every cell every frame at a large zoom is cheap (only the visible ones), but at the 12 px threshold the visible cells number in the tens of thousands. If panning stutters in the windowed run, chunk the bake rather than raise the threshold, and say so in the PR.
  - **Generated art can drift.** An image model may ignore the size, the palette or the transparency. The conform step makes the format right, but not the look: the visual review settles the look, and a tile the user rejects is regenerated in a rework round, not hand-painted.
  - **The key and the cost.** The OpenRouter key is the owner's (`OPENROUTER_API_KEY` or `assets-generator.local.ini`, both outside the repository); never print, log or commit it. Never put an original screenshot or tile into a prompt.
