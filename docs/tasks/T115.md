#### T115 In `classical-faithful`, recruiting and fortifying can put the treasury into debt

- **Design milestone**: none. It is a correction for bug [#549](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial_conquest_2/issues/549), found by stage 2 of the evidence pipeline (PR [#550](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial_conquest_2/pull/550)). It was planned in the triage of 2026-10-03 by the user's decision, on the user's decision of 2026-10-01 that only `classical-faithful` stops refusing. **Labels**: `phase:2 lane:engine`

- **Scope**: two handlers refuse an order the treasury cannot cover: `RecruitStandingUnitCommandHandler` (`recruitment.insufficient-treasury`) and `OrderCityCommandHandler` (`city-order.insufficient-treasury`). The original checks no balance in either:
  - **Recruitment:** `TArmyRecruits.RecruitUnit` (`0x454E78`) refuses only a full 40-slot table, a mobilisation already at 100%, and a non-capital town whose fortification word is ≤ 74. It then takes `troops / 200 × cost(type)` from the treasury (`+0x438`), with no money check, so recruiting can take the treasury below zero **[derived: decompiled, [`2026-09-29-which-cities-may-recruit-and-troop-amounts.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/main/docs/reports/2026-09-29-which-cities-may-recruit-and-troop-amounts.md) §2]**.
  - **Fortification:** the Fortify dialog's `OK` (`0x4407E4`) does `treasury −= population × points`, "paid now, in full", with no money check, so the treasury can go negative **[derived: decompiled, [`2026-09-29-fortification-orders-cost-rate-and-the-100-bug.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/main/docs/reports/2026-09-29-fortification-orders-cost-rate-and-the-100-bug.md) §1]**.
  - **The user's decision of 2026-10-01** (PR #550's comment, recorded in `game-design.md`'s Recruitment refusals and `design-audit.md` §1.4): `classical-faithful` allows that debt, for recruiting and fortifying alike; `improved` keeps refusing an order the treasury can't cover.
  - **The flag.** A preset difference is a `RulesetFlags` member, as every other one is. The new one is `unaffordableRecruitAndFortify`, an enum with the values `allowDebt` and `refuse` [designed: the name, after `bugPolicySiegeRatioClamp`'s enum style; it names the two orders so that no other order's treasury check reads it]. `classical-faithful` is `allowDebt` and `improved` is `refuse`, each with a `_provenance` note citing the reports above and the user's decision.
  - **`toy-ruleset.json` is `refuse`**, unlike every other flag, which it shares with `classical-faithful` [designed: the user's decision of 2026-10-03; `classical-faithful` is `allowDebt` and `improved` is `refuse`]. The CLI demo's toy nation orders a fortification and a recruitment with 0 talents (`demo.golden.txt` ~:113 and ~:137). With `allowDebt` it would fall 320 talents into debt, and with the toy's wealth of 400 the debt line is 0, so `north` would be deposed at its next turn start and, as the only human seat, end the demo at its first `end`. So the exporter writes `allowDebt` and its note into `classical-faithful.json` itself, the way it writes `id`, `name` and `description`, and the toy keeps today's refusal for every test that loads it.
  - **What an order costs when the treasury is short:** the same as when it is not. The full cost is taken when the order is placed, never per tick, and the treasury may end below zero. Nothing else about the order changes: the slot is appended and mobilisation rises (recruitment), and the fortification word takes the order (fortify). The reports read this from the code; their live runs paid both orders from a positive treasury (2,200 → 2,080 for a recruitment, 2,200 → 1,714 for a fortification) **[Wine candidate: the fortification report, §8 for the recruitment and §6 for the fortification]**.
  - **No other order changes.** Buying supply, building, repairing or scuttling a fleet, hiring a mercenary and every purse transfer keep their own checks in both presets: the decision and the reports name these two orders only.
- **Done when**:
  1. `OrdersIntoDebtTests`, under `classical-faithful` (or the toy ruleset `with` the flag set to `allowDebt`): a Roman nation with less in its treasury than a recruitment's cost places the order. The treasury ends at exactly its old value minus `StandingRecruitmentCost.InitialCost`, below zero, the slot is appended and `MobilizedPercent` rises as for an affordable order. It fails on `main` and passes after.
  2. The same for `order-city … fortify`: accepted with too little money, the treasury falls by exactly `population × points` (through the ruleset's cost per point) to below zero, and the city's fortification word holds the order.
  3. Under `improved` (or the flag set to `refuse`), both orders are refused with their existing rejections and the state is unchanged. The existing `RecruitStandingUnitCommandHandlerTests` treasury test passes unchanged on the toy ruleset.
  4. An order whose issuing nation is unknown is still refused by `OrderCityCommandHandler`, under both values.
  5. **The flag's values.** `classical-faithful.json` has `allowDebt` and `improved.json` and `toy-ruleset.json` have `refuse`, each with a `_provenance` note; `ExportedRulesetTests.Flags_reproduce_the_original_faithfully` asserts `allowDebt`. `ImprovedPresetTests`' list of expected differences gains exactly the new path. `ExportScriptReproducibilityTests` passes, so a second export run reproduces `classical-faithful.json` byte for byte, and `NoToyProvenanceWordingTests` passes.
  6. `ScenarioAuthoringDocumentationTest` passes with the new row in `docs/scenario-authoring.md`.
  7. **Nothing printed changes.** `demo.golden.txt`, `success.golden.txt`, `success-fleet.golden.txt` and `seat-rome.golden.txt` are unchanged byte for byte, and no seeded AI test, soak log or golden changes.
  8. `dotnet build IC2.sln` and `dotnet test IC2.sln` are green, and the diff lists only Owns paths.
- **Hazards**:
  - **Debt now reaches deposition.** In `classical-faithful`, a human who over-recruits or over-fortifies goes below the debt line and is deposed at the start of their next turn (`Deposition.ShouldFallAtHumanTurnStart`, T87). That is the original's rule and this task's intended outcome. Add no warning or refusal for it; a UI warning would be a design decision for the user.
  - **The AI is unaffected in practice.** `AiEconomyPhase` spends nothing once its budget is ≤ 0 (~:82) and checks the treasury itself before recruiting (~:276) and fortifying (~:390). If a seeded AI outcome changes anyway, stop and report.
  - **The exporter writes the one flag.** If it cannot do so without breaking `ExportScriptReproducibilityTests` or `ExportScriptToyGuardTests`, stop and report; don't change the toy's value to get round it.
  - **The recruitment refusals the original does have are not this task's.** The capital-or-75% rule and the mobilisation-at-100% refusal are [#515](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial_conquest_2/issues/515)'s, in the same handler. Don't add them here; #515's correction, once planned, is never in flight with this task.
  - **A live run with a short treasury has not been seen.** Both reports read the missing check from the code, and no run has yet placed either order from a treasury too small to cover it. If one disagrees, that is a follow-up.
  - The New Game preset cards (`godot/UI/RulesetPresets.cs`) list five fixed dimensions (T24) and do not change here.
