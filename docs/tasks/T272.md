Issue [#1710](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial_conquest_2/issues/1710).
Separate correction investigation: the unchanged main-branch native glyph
verifier reports authored weak/middling army alpha heights `[32,32]` against
its `[22,27]` expectation. T271 reproduces the same result without changing
those images, while the thirty canonical battle BMPs match their generator.

Establish the current authored marker encoding and the applicable T187/owner
stature contract before changing either art or verification. Alpha bounds may
not measure foreground stature in an opaque/encoded marker. Do not assume
which side is wrong, skip the assertion, regenerate the accepted Gemini six
or claim human visual acceptance from a script.

Own the measured marker-conformance correction and its focused test in a
separate PR, with the relevant T187 provenance and actual/native-size evidence.
No release placement is implied. T271 certifies only its optional assets and
the unchanged canonical battle glyph bytes, not this pre-existing discrepancy.

## Format evidence and bounded correction

The owner's 2026-10-21 recolour-template design is recorded in
`scripts/generate-strategic-markers.py` and the production asset tests:
authored markers have an opaque purple background with white/blue/black motifs.
Their full alpha bounds therefore describe the field, not the soldier.
Measured weak/middling foreground heights are 25/28 px; the transparent native
placeholders measure 22/27 px. T187 requires distinct builds, not shared pixel
geometry across these source families. Its old Gemini-source wording predates
this later owner design; this correction changes no accepted image.

Measure non-purple opaque foreground for the authored format and retain the
exact 22/27 placeholder contract. Reject empty/equal/reversed authored heights
and malformed BMPs. Retain all existing byte-equality checks, with no asset
rewrite or claim of new human visual acceptance. Run
`python scripts/test-unit-glyph-verifier.py` and
`python scripts/generate-unit-glyphs.py --verify`; the former exercises the real
measurement/verification with equal-height and empty-motif negative controls.
The correction is included in the v0.9 release-verification work.
