- **Scope**: T106 caps the receiving army's supply at `troops div 100 + 1` and refuses past it (`SupplyExceedsCapacity`), and caps a merged purse at 1,000, spilling the excess to the treasury. The original's `OK` refuses nothing and rebalances supply, and its merge caps nothing. Every rule below is **[confirmed: code, [`2026-10-03-army-to-army-ok-supply-rebalancing.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/main/docs/reports/2026-10-03-army-to-army-ok-supply-rebalancing.md) (research `a380a8e`), `TArmyToArmy_OK` `:44572–44649`]** unless marked.
  - **One command is one dialog `OK`, both ways** [designed: the user's decision of 2026-10-03]. In the original, A is the army selected when the order was given and B is its partner, and one `OK` commits the whole dialog. Here A is the command's first named army, the selected one, and B the second. The command carries both directions:
    `army-transfer <selected> <partner> [units=<i,j,…>] [supply=<tons>] [money=<talents>] [back-units=<i,j,…>] [back-supply=<tons>] [back-money=<talents>]`
    `units`, `supply` and `money` go from A to B, as today. The `back-` options go from B to A, with the same syntax and the same parser rules (each option once, whole numbers, non-negative unit indexes). An order with only the A-to-B options is exactly T106's order, so every existing line still parses.
  - **The amounts are checked on the state before the order** [derived: the dialog clamps each click to what the giver holds]. Each side's unit indexes are its own, before the order. Each side gives at most the supply and money it holds before the order. The 20-unit and 100,000-troop caps apply to each army's composition **after both directions**. An army that receives money ends with at most 1,000 talents (`PurseCapExceeded`). Any refusal leaves the state unchanged, so a dialog never commits in part.
  - **Then everything is committed at once**, both directions' units, supply and money, and the steps below run **once**, as the original's single `OK` does.
  - **After the units, supply and money are moved**, `OK` runs these steps in this order:
    1. `capA = troops(A) div 100` (`SupplyCapacity.ArmyCapacityTons`; an empty army has capacity 0, because `FUN_0044A698` returns 1 for 0 troops). If `supply(A) > capA`, A's excess goes to B and A is left at `capA`.
    2. `capB = troops(B) div 100`. If `supply(B) > capB` (including what step 1 pushed), B's excess goes back to A and B is left at `capB`.
    3. If A has no units: B takes all of A's supply and **all of A's money, with no cap**, and A is deleted (as T106 already does, with its carried-fleet back-reference sweep).
    4. If B has no units: the same the other way, A taking all of B's supply and money, uncapped, and B is deleted. `back-units` that moves all of B's units reaches this step.
  - **So supply is conserved exactly**, and when both armies end over capacity, B ends at exactly `capB` and A keeps everything else, above its own capacity. When one army is emptied, the survivor holds all the supply and all the money, uncapped. The rebalancing runs on **every** accepted transfer, a money-only or unit-only one included.
  - **Nothing in `OK` refuses.** `SupplyExceedsCapacity` goes: any amount up to what the giving army holds is accepted, and the rebalance places it. The dialog's supply room, `max(0, troops(receiver) div 100 − supply(receiver) + 1)` (`FUN_00442310`), bounds only the dialog's spinner; it is T111's limit, not this command's.
  - **Money is never rebalanced**, and the merge adds it with no cap: the merge's `PurseAccounting.Credit` call and its spill to the treasury go. `PurseCapExceeded` **stays**: it is the dialog's money stepper, `min(step, 1000 − money(receiver), money(giver))` (`:44044–44088`), the only bound the original puts on money moved between two surviving armies.
  - **Everything else stands**: the distance-1 gate, ownership, the 20-unit and 100,000-troop caps (the dialog's per-unit Transfer refusals, `:44091–44250`), and the amount checks.
  - **Both presets.** No user decision makes this a preset difference, and `ArmyTransferCommandHandler` reads no ruleset flag today, so `improved` does not diverge deliberately. The rule applies in both.
- **Done when**:
  1. **The report's worked example.** A and B each hold 30,000 troops (capacity 300) and 500 t. A transfer of 10 talents from A to B leaves A at 700 t and B at 300 t (`S = 1,000`, B at `capB`, A holding the rest). The same armies with the roles swapped (B named first) leave B at 700 t and A at 300 t. It fails on `main` (no rebalancing) and passes after.
  2. **One two-way order, the review's example** (PR #632's review, R4). A holds 30,000 troops and 500 t; B holds 70,000 troops, among them a 10,000-troop unit, and 0 t. `army-transfer A B supply=450 back-units=<that unit>` is **accepted** as one command. Afterwards A holds 40,000 troops and 50 t, B holds 60,000 troops and 450 t, both within capacity (400 and 600), and the rebalance moves nothing. Submitted instead as two one-way commands, unit first, the second would be refused for want of supply; this test pins that the single command is not.
  3. **Two-way merges and refusals.** A `back-units` order that moves all of B's units into A leaves A with all of B's supply and money, uncapped, and deletes B. A two-way order in which one direction fails a check (for example `back-supply` above what B holds, or a unit cap on the final composition) is refused, and neither direction is applied.
  4. **One rebalance, not one per direction** (PR #641's review, R8). A and B each hold 30,000 troops (capacity 300) and 500 t, and one order exchanges a 10,000-troop unit each way (`army-transfer A B units=<i> back-units=<j>`). Both armies survive and both end over capacity before the rebalance. `ArmyTransferCommandTests` asserts **A 700 t, B 300 t**: one `OK` rebalance (A capped first, its 200 t pushed to B, then B capped and its 400 t pushed back). A rebalance after each direction would leave A 300 t and B 700 t, so this test tells them apart.
  5. **The parser.** `ArmyTransferParserTests` pins each `back-` option, a repeated option, a negative or non-numeric value and an unknown key (each prints the usage line), and that T106's one-way form parses as before.
  6. **One over capacity.** A transfer that leaves only A over its capacity pushes exactly the excess to B. One that leaves only B over pushes exactly the excess back to A. A transfer that leaves both within capacity moves nothing extra. In each case the two armies' supply sums to what it was before.
  7. **No supply refusal.** A transfer of more supply than the receiver's `troops div 100 + 1` is accepted, and the rebalance places it. T106's `Transfer_SupplyOnePastTheReceiverCapacity_IsRejected` is replaced by this test.
  8. **The uncapped merge.** A transfer that empties A into B, where the pooled purse is above 1,000 and the pooled supply is above B's capacity, leaves B with all the money and all the supply, and the treasury unchanged. T106's `Transfer_ThatEmptiesOneArmy_SpillsTheExcessPurseToTheTreasury` is replaced by this test.
  9. **The money stepper stays.** `Transfer_MoneyOnePastThePurseCap_IsRejected` and `Transfer_MoneyExactlyAtThePurseCap_IsAccepted` pass unchanged.
  10. **Every other T106 test stands**, and `CommandCoverageTests` passes with the success golden's `army-transfer` line still accepted, and `SuccessScriptTests` passes.
  11. **Provenance.** `ArmyTransferCommand.cs`'s remarks name `TArmyToArmy_OK` and the report, and `git grep -n '\[open\]' src/IC2.Engine/Armies/Commands/ArmyTransferCommand.cs` finds nothing.
  12. `dotnet build IC2.sln` and `dotnet test IC2.sln` are green, and the diff lists only Owns paths.
