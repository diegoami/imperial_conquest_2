- **Scope**: the clone's turn order is the world file's fixed default, the DAT's nation order with Rome first. The original shuffles it at New Game. Every rule below is **[confirmed: code, [`2026-10-03-new-game-turn-order-shuffle.md`](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial-conquest-2-research/blob/main/docs/reports/2026-10-03-new-game-turn-order-shuffle.md) (research `871ca91`), `FUN_00448AA4`]** unless marked.
  - **The algorithm.** Start from the world's `turnOrder`, which is the DAT's nation order: index 0 is Rome. For `i = 0, 1, …, n − 1` in ascending order, draw `j = Random(n)` and swap `order[i]` with `order[j]`. In the original `n` is 16, so it always makes exactly 16 draws, and a draw of `j = i` swaps nothing. The first nation of the result is the active seat, at position 0.
  - **It is not Fisher–Yates, on purpose.** `j` is drawn from every slot each time, not from `i..n − 1`, so the `nⁿ` equally likely draw sequences do not spread evenly over the `n!` orders. That bias is the original's, and the clone keeps it.
  - **The generator is the clone's own** [designed: the user's decision of 2026-10-03, algorithm parity and not seed-for-seed parity]. The draws come from an `IRng` stream derived from the game's starting seed (`SplitMix64Rng.ForStream(state.RandomSeed, "new-game.turn-order")` [designed name]), after any seed override (the CLI's `--seed`, the soak's seed). The call does not advance `RandomSeed`, so no other draw moves. **Seed 12345 does not give the original's order for seed 12345.** The original runs Delphi's LCG, after 436 earlier draws (mercenary fill, weather overlay, leaders), which the clone does not reproduce.
  - **Fixed for the whole game.** Nothing after New Game rewrites the order. An eliminated nation keeps its slot, and the human's choice of nation does not change the order: the original shuffles before the seat form opens.
  - **Saved and restored as today.** `GameState.TurnOrder` and `ActiveSeatIndex` are already in the save. Resuming a save (`GameSession`'s resume path, T95) never shuffles again.
  - **The preset gate.** A new flag, `newGameTurnOrder` [designed name], has two values:
    - `shuffle` in `classical-faithful`;
    - `fixed` in `improved` [designed: the user's decision of 2026-10-02].
    - `toy-ruleset.json` carries `fixed`, so the toy demo and every toy session keep their order. The exporter writes `shuffle` and its note into `classical-faithful.json` itself, as T115 does for its flag [designed: this plan, after T115's precedent].
  - **Where it runs.** `NewGameTurnOrder.Apply(state, ruleset)` is called by `GameSession`'s New Game constructor and by `AiGameRunner`, the two places a game starts. `GameStateFactory.CreateInitial` stays the scenario's opening state in the world's order, so state-level tests built from it are unaffected.

The draws are **[derived: this plan]**: they replay the report's generator (`RandSeed := RandSeed × $08088405 + 1`, `Random(n) = (RandSeed × n) shr 32`) from each seed past its 436, 217 and 641 earlier draws, and each replay reproduces its order. It fails on `main` (no shuffle) and passes after.
  2. **The bias is the original's.** Over all 27 draw sequences of a 3-nation order `a b c`, the shuffle gives `abc` 4 times, `acb` 5, `bac` 5, `bca` 5, `cab` 4 and `cba` 4. A Fisher–Yates shuffle would give each order 6 of 27 equally, and this test fails on it.
  3. **The preset gate.** A `classical-faithful` New Game through `GameSession` has a permutation of the world's 16 nations as its order, the same for every human seat chosen and for the same seed, and its active seat is the order's first nation. Two different `--seed` values give their own orders. An `improved` New Game and a toy New Game keep the world's order.
  4. **Fixed and saved.** After a full round (every seat's `end`), the order is unchanged and the calendar has advanced exactly once. A `SaveManager` round trip, then a resume, keeps the order and the active seat, and draws nothing.
  5. **The rulesets.** `classical-faithful.json` has `shuffle`, and `improved.json` and `toy-ruleset.json` have `fixed`. `ImprovedPresetTests` gains exactly the new path, `ExportedRulesetTests` asserts `shuffle`, `ExportScriptReproducibilityTests` passes, and `ScenarioAuthoringDocumentationTest` passes.

- **Hazards**:
  - **Seeded outcomes change, for `classical-faithful` only.** Every New Game on the classical scenario now starts with a shuffled seat order, and the AI seats ahead of the human play before the human's first turn (`AdvanceToHumanSeat`). So the Owns tests, checks, goldens and AI measurements above can shift. A test that is not about the turn order pins the order it needs: it builds its session with the ruleset `with` the flag set to `fixed`, or reads the active seat instead of assuming Rome. Don't weaken an assertion to pass. If an AI invariant (0 rejected commands, the stall bounds) fails, stop and report.
  - **The success script's `--seed 3` was chosen for its outcomes** (`SuccessScriptTests`' remarks, "Why seed 3"). If the shuffled order breaks an accepted line, a different seed may be chosen, with the reason for each line re-checked in the PR. If no seed keeps every line accepted, stop and report.
  - **The Godot checks** build `classical-mediterranean` sessions for a chosen human seat. With the shuffle, AI seats may play first and move units the checks expect in place. Adjust only each check's session setup, as above, and run the checks one at a time.
  - **`AiGameRunner` feeds `tools/AutoResolveTournament`.** Its recorded results in `docs/investigations/auto-resolve-tournament-results.md` were measured on the fixed order. Don't re-run or edit them here; say so in the PR.
  - **The round boundary.** The original's weekly tick fires when the position wraps to 0, so with a shuffled order the round starts at `order[0]`, whoever that is. Check that `SeatRotationSystem` and the calendar tick key on the position, not on Rome. If they key on a nation, stop and report.
  - **A world with other than 16 nations** draws `Random(n)` with its own `n` [derived: the original only ever has 16]. The toy worlds carry `fixed`, so this matters only to a modder's ruleset.
