- **Scope**:

  - **#199: no diplomacy handler rejects an eliminated counterparty.** `ProposeTradeCommandHandler`, `ProposeAllianceCommandHandler` and `AcceptPendingOfferCommandHandler` never check `NationState.Eliminated` on the other side; `CommandDispatcher` checks only the issuer. A nation eliminated between the turn-start offer roll and the acceptance still gets a `Trade` relation written to it, and T17 makes that path live. PR #364's review (N2) proved that `DeclareWarCommandHandler` and `MakePeaceCommandHandler` also accept an eliminated counterparty and change its relation.
  - **The original's rule, found at review.** Both elimination paths, `FUN_0044BED8` and `FUN_0044C528`, call the relation setter `FUN_00449B40(eliminated, n, 0)` for every nation. Given state 0, the setter writes a cooldown by the previous state: trade −8, alliance −24, war −18. Sources: research `decompiled-ai-offers-to-human-seats.md:43` (**[confirmed]** across all 22 setter call sites), `decompiled-diplomacy-peace-terms-and-instant-battles.md:28–34`, and the local dump `%LOCALAPPDATA%\ReTools\all_app_functions.txt` at ~:50381–50391 and ~:50674–50677. **The user decided on 2026-09-25**, replacing the 2026-09-23 "kept as history" decision, that:
    - on elimination, every relation the eliminated nation holds is **reset as the original resets it**, in every preset, through `RelationTransitions.BreakToPeace`'s mapping, which is the engine's form of the setter's state 0; nothing is deleted;
    - all five handlers (propose trade, propose alliance, accept offer, declare war, make peace) **reject** an eliminated counterparty, with **one shared typed rejection**, the way T19 factored `RelationTransitions.IsAtWarWithAnyone`. The rejection is `[designed]`: the original's UI never offers an eliminated nation as a target, which is not the same evidence.
