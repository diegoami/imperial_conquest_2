- **Scope**:
  - **Protects**: `classical-faithful`'s tactical battle, unchanged in every rule, draw and value (the golden master, T129, still passes, and every T123–T125 test still passes under `classical-faithful`); and that every `improved` difference is one ruleset switch or value, read from the ruleset and nowhere else. Forbidden: a difference keyed on the ruleset's id or name instead of a field; a classical default that changes; a draw added to a classical battle; a retreat-losses or cap number written into the code or invented without the design note and the user's confirmation; any change to the instant path's `combat.onDefeat` scatter rule.
  - **The switches** [designed: the field names; the user's decisions of 2026-10-04 on #496], each defaulting to the original's behaviour:
    - `midBattleSave`: `anyTime` (classical) or `halfRoundStart` (improved). Under `halfRoundStart`, `save` during a battle is refused, with its message, after any order of the current half-round, and allowed before the first (T125's `atHalfRoundStart`); the load then takes T125's **exact-restore** path instead of `Resume`, so the counter is not incremented, moves and targets are as saved, and the cap and the slow-advance window do not move. T125 owns the path; this task owns only the switch that selects it.
    - `shotsNeverNegative`: pass 2's shooting loop stops at 0 shots.
    - `wideArithmetic`: the products are computed in 64 bits (no 32-bit wrap; the shot bound's `min` in 32 bits).
    - `placementStacks`: `true` (classical) or `false`: a formation block of more than nine units continues into the next empty cell of the side's home rows, in row order, and a unit with no empty home cell keeps its copy-in position.
    - `slowAdvance`: `true` or `false` (improved: a computer side moves its full moves from the start).
    - `halfRoundCap`: 0 for none (classical) or a number (improved): the battle ends after that many half-rounds. **The number and the outcome at the cap are both posed in the design note** for the user's confirmation; the code implements the confirmed outcome.
    - `retreatLosses` (and whatever survival switch the confirmed note needs): after a loss, the loser's remnant takes the retreat losses and is then placed by `combat.onDefeat`'s existing scatter rule (`ScatterPlacement`, unchanged), or destroyed when nothing is left or it is boxed in. **What the remnant is made of is posed in the design note**, not decided here.
  - **The design note.** The PR's first commit adds to its description (not to a file) a note that poses, for the user's confirmation: **the cap** (its number, and its outcome: for example no winner, the attacker withdrawing, both armies keeping their survivors, with or without promotion, transfers, unity and a news line); **the remnant** (whether the loser's units that left the board by rout, by surrender, or both, keep the troops they had when they left, or some fraction of them, and so form the remnant, or whether only an army that loses by the cap or another rule has one); and **the retreat losses**: the per-type pursuit weights (light and heavy cavalry, archers, infantry), the base fraction, the scaling against the remnant's size, the cap, whether a draw adds variance (and if so, its place in the battle's stream: after the promotions and the peace draw), per unit or per army, whether the winner's routed units rejoin, and whether a capped battle's withdrawal costs the attacker retreat losses. It grounds each proposal where it can (for example, the classical shot and melee caps as an upper bound) and marks the rest `[designed]`. The values and rules go into `improved.json`, the code and `game-design.md` only after the user confirms them on the PR.
